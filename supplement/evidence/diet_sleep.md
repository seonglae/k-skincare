# Diet, Meal Timing, and Sleep Onset — Evidence Review

**Clinical context:** Adult patient with delayed sleep phase (typical onset ≥2-3am), wants to phase-advance.
**Date:** 2026-05-18
**Approach:** RCTs, systematic reviews, meta-analyses on diet/supplement/behavioral interventions for sleep onset latency (SOL), total sleep time (TST), and circadian phase. Verified via PubMed.

---

## Bottom Line (Top 3 Highest-Evidence Interventions)

For an adult with delayed sleep-wake phase (typical onset ≥2-3am), the strongest evidence supports a **stacked behavioural-first protocol**: (1) **morning bright light (≥2,500–10,000 lux for 30–60 min on waking)** combined with progressive sleep-schedule advancement — this is the only intervention class that meaningfully shifts the endogenous circadian phase (Sletten 2018, Saxvig 2014); (2) **caffeine cut-off ≥6 h before target bedtime** (Drake 2013 RCT showed ~1 h of total sleep lost from caffeine taken 6 h pre-bed); and (3) **glycine 3 g 30–60 min pre-bed**, the most reproducible dietary supplement signal with PSG-confirmed reductions in sleep-onset latency (Yamadera 2007, Bannai 2012). Magnesium (~17 min ↓ SOL pooled), tart cherry juice, kiwifruit, and tryptophan are useful adjuncts. **Melatonin is the gold-standard chronobiotic for DSWPD but is prescription-only in the UK** — it normally fits as 0.5 mg taken ~5–6 h before current sleep onset (e.g. ~21:00 if onset is ~03:00), and would be the recommended Rx add-on if behavioural measures fail (Sletten 2018).

## 1. Tryptophan / 5-HTP

**1.1 Sutanto CN, et al. (2022).** *The impact of tryptophan supplementation on sleep quality: a systematic review, meta-analysis, and meta-regression.* **Nutrition Reviews 80(2):306–316.** PMID: 33942088. DOI: 10.1093/nutrit/nuab027.
- *Design / N:* SR + MA of RCTs; 4 studies pooled (n ≈ small, dose 250 mg – 15 g).
- *Intervention vs comparator:* Oral L-tryptophan vs placebo.
- *Outcome / effect:* Wake-after-sleep-onset reduced by ~81 min/g of tryptophan; SOL trend toward ~15 min reduction; doses **≥1 g** more effective than <1 g. No clear TST effect.
- *Quality:* **B** (small RCT base, heterogeneous doses).
- *Clinical takeaway:* 1–3 g L-tryptophan 30–60 min pre-bed is a reasonable adjunct, particularly for maintenance insomnia; SSRIs/MAOIs are contraindications.

## 2. Glycine 3 g pre-bed

**2.1 Yamadera W, Inagawa K, et al. (2007).** *Glycine ingestion improves subjective sleep quality in human volunteers, correlating with polysomnographic changes.* **Sleep and Biological Rhythms 5(2):126–131.** DOI: 10.1111/j.1479-8425.2007.00262.x.
- *Design / N:* Placebo-controlled crossover; n = 11 self-reported poor sleepers, PSG-confirmed.
- *Intervention:* 3 g glycine vs placebo, 30 min before bed.
- *Outcome:* Significantly shortened **PSG latency to stage-2 sleep (P = 0.01)** and to slow-wave sleep (P = 0.019); improved subjective sleep quality and sleep efficiency; REM preserved.
- *Quality:* **B+** (objective PSG endpoints, but small n).
- *Takeaway:* Best-evidenced single dietary amino-acid intervention for SOL.

**2.2 Bannai M, Kawai N, Ono K, Nakahara K, Murakami N (2012).** *The effects of glycine on subjective daytime performance in partially sleep-restricted healthy volunteers.* **Frontiers in Neurology 3:61.** PMID: 22529837. DOI: 10.3389/fneur.2012.00061.
- *Design / N:* Randomised crossover; n = 7 healthy males, sleep restricted 25%.
- *Intervention:* Glycine 3 g vs placebo, 30 min pre-bed × 3 nights.
- *Outcome:* Reduced next-day fatigue/daytime sleepiness; improved cognitive task performance.
- *Quality:* **B−** (very small n).
- *Takeaway:* Supports glycine's daytime carry-over benefit when sleep is curtailed — relevant during phase-advance attempts.

## 3. Magnesium

**3.1 Abbasi B, Kimiagar M, et al. (2012).** *The effect of magnesium supplementation on primary insomnia in elderly: a double-blind placebo-controlled clinical trial.* **J Res Med Sci 17(12):1161–1169.** PMID: 23853635. PMCID: PMC3703169.
- *Design / N:* Double-blind RCT; n = 46 elderly with primary insomnia.
- *Intervention:* Mg 500 mg/day × 8 weeks vs placebo.
- *Outcome:* ↓ ISI (P = 0.006), ↓ SOL (P = 0.02), ↑ sleep efficiency (P = 0.03), ↑ serum melatonin (P = 0.007), ↓ serum cortisol (P = 0.008).
- *Quality:* **B** (RCT but elderly-only).
- *Takeaway:* Highest-quality single RCT supporting Mg → endogenous melatonin elevation.

**3.2 Mah J, Pitre T (2021).** *Oral magnesium supplementation for insomnia in older adults: a Systematic Review & Meta-Analysis.* **BMC Complement Med Ther 21(1):125.** PMID: 33865376. PMCID: PMC8053283. DOI: 10.1186/s12906-021-03297-z.
- *Design:* SR/MA of 3 RCTs, n = 151 older adults.
- *Outcome:* Pooled SOL **−17.36 min** vs placebo; TST +16 min (NS). All trials moderate-to-high risk of bias; evidence grade low/very low.
- *Quality:* **B−** (effect direction consistent; certainty low).
- *Takeaway:* Effect size clinically meaningful but evidence base thin; 200–400 mg elemental Mg (glycinate / citrate / threonate) is reasonable empirically.

**3.3 Boyle NB, Lawton C, Dye L (2017).** *The effects of magnesium supplementation on subjective anxiety and stress — a systematic review.* **Nutrients 9(5):429.** PMID: 28445426. DOI: 10.3390/nu9050429.
- *Design:* SR of 18 trials.
- *Outcome:* Suggestive benefit on anxiety in anxiety-vulnerable samples; methodological heterogeneity high.
- *Quality:* **C+.**
- *Takeaway:* Plausible anxiolytic pathway that may indirectly aid sleep onset in tense individuals.

## 4. Carbohydrate / Glycemic Index & Sleep Onset

**4.1 Afaghi A, O'Connor H, Chow CM (2007).** *High-glycemic-index carbohydrate meals shorten sleep onset.* **Am J Clin Nutr 85(2):426–430.** PMID: 17284739. DOI: 10.1093/ajcn/85.2.426.
- *Design / N:* Crossover; n = 12 healthy men 18–35.
- *Intervention:* Isocaloric high-GI (jasmine rice, GI = 109) vs low-GI (mahatma, GI = 50) meal at 1 h vs 4 h pre-bed.
- *Outcome:* High-GI meal **4 h before bed → SOL 9.0 ± 6.2 min** vs low-GI **17.5 ± 6.2 min (P = 0.009)**; high-GI 1 h before bed less effective than 4 h.
- *Quality:* **B+** (clean isocaloric design; small n).
- *Takeaway:* Eating dinner ~4 h before target bedtime, with a high-GI carb (e.g., white rice), halves SOL — directly applicable.

## 5. Caffeine Cut-Off

**5.1 Drake C, Roehrs T, Shambroom J, Roth T (2013).** *Caffeine effects on sleep taken 0, 3, or 6 hours before going to bed.* **J Clin Sleep Med 9(11):1195–1200.** PMID: 24235903. PMCID: PMC3805807. DOI: 10.5664/jcsm.3170.
- *Design / N:* Double-blind, placebo-controlled within-subject; n = 12 healthy normal sleepers.
- *Intervention:* 400 mg caffeine vs placebo at bedtime, 3 h, or 6 h before bed × 4 nights.
- *Outcome:* Even caffeine taken **6 h before bed reduced objectively measured TST by >1 hour**; all caffeine times significantly disrupted sleep vs placebo.
- *Quality:* **A−** (objective sleep monitoring, dose-controlled).
- *Takeaway:* Hard rule — no caffeine after ~14:00 if targeting 23:00 bedtime. Highest behavioural ROI per effort.

## 6. Alcohol & Sleep Architecture

**6.1 He S, Hasler BP, Chakravorty S (2024).** *The effect of alcohol on subsequent sleep in healthy adults: a systematic review and meta-analysis.* **Sleep Med Rev 79:101871.** PMID: 39631226. DOI: 10.1016/j.smrv.2024.101871.
- *Design:* SR/MA of 27 controlled alcohol-administration studies.
- *Outcome:* REM-sleep onset latency +18 min; **REM duration ↓11.3 min per drink condition**; per 1 g/kg dose: REM latency +30.1 min, REM duration −40.4 min. TST/SE effects uncertain.
- *Quality:* **A−.**
- *Takeaway:* Alcohol may *feel* like it helps onset but fragments REM and circadian regulation — avoid as a sleep aid, especially during a phase-advance attempt.

## 7. Meal Timing / Late-Night Eating

**7.1 Crispim CA, Zimberg IZ, dos Reis BG, et al. (2011).** *Relationship between food intake and sleep pattern in healthy individuals.* **J Clin Sleep Med 7(6):659–664.** PMID: 22171206. PMCID: PMC3227713. DOI: 10.5664/jcsm.1476.
- *Design / N:* Observational with PSG; n = 52 healthy adults + 3-day food diary.
- *Outcome:* Higher caloric and fat intake near bedtime → longer SOL and lower sleep efficiency, especially in women; nocturnal fat ↑ REM latency in men.
- *Quality:* **C+** (observational).
- *Takeaway:* Stop eating ~3–4 h before target bedtime, avoid late high-fat meals — consistent with #4 Afaghi.

## 8. Tart Cherry (Montmorency, *Prunus cerasus*)

**8.1 Howatson G, Bell PG, Tallent J, Middleton B, McHugh MP, Ellis J (2012).** *Effect of tart cherry juice (Prunus cerasus) on melatonin levels and enhanced sleep quality.* **Eur J Nutr 51(8):909–916.** PMID: 22038497. DOI: 10.1007/s00394-011-0263-7.
- *Design / N:* Randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover; n = 20 healthy adults.
- *Intervention:* 30 mL tart cherry concentrate × 2/day × 7 days vs placebo.
- *Outcome:* ↑ TST +34 min, ↑ sleep efficiency +5–6%, ↑ time in bed +25 min; urinary 6-sulfatoxymelatonin +17%.
- *Quality:* **B+.**
- *Takeaway:* Modest but real TST gain; melatonin increase is small (≈85 μg/day vs Rx 0.5–5 mg), so mechanism likely also tryptophan/anti-inflammatory.

**8.2 Pigeon WR, Carr M, Gorman C, Perlis ML (2010).** *Effects of a tart cherry juice beverage on the sleep of older adults with insomnia: a pilot study.* **J Med Food 13(3):579–583.** PMID: 20438325. DOI: 10.1089/jmf.2009.0096.
- *Design / N:* Randomised double-blind crossover; n = 15 older adults with chronic insomnia.
- *Outcome:* vs placebo, significant ↓ in insomnia severity (WASO); SOL/TST changes pre-to-post but NS vs placebo. Effect size ≥ valerian, ~ comparable to some melatonin studies.
- *Quality:* **B−.**
- *Takeaway:* Useful low-risk adjunct; effect smaller than CBT-I or hypnotics.

## 9. Kiwifruit

**9.1 Lin HH, Tsai PS, Fang SC, Liu JF (2011).** *Effect of kiwifruit consumption on sleep quality in adults with sleep problems.* **Asia Pac J Clin Nutr 20(2):169–174.** PMID: 21669584.
- *Design / N:* Self-controlled trial; n = 24 adults (mostly female) with sleep disturbance; 4 weeks.
- *Intervention:* 2 kiwifruits 1 h before bed nightly.
- *Outcome:* SOL ↓35.4%, WASO ↓28.9%, TST ↑13.4%, SE ↑5.4%, PSQI ↓42.4%.
- *Quality:* **C+** (no placebo arm; large pre-post effect).
- *Takeaway:* Cheap, low-risk; serotonin + folate + vitamin C hypothesis. Worth trying empirically given the safety/cost profile.

## 10. Apigenin / Chamomile

**10.1 Hieu TH, et al. (2019).** *Therapeutic efficacy and safety of chamomile for state anxiety, generalized anxiety disorder, insomnia, and sleep quality: a systematic review and meta-analysis.* **Phytother Res 33(6):1604–1615.** DOI: 10.1002/ptr.6349.
- *Design:* SR/MA of 12 RCTs/quasi-RCTs.
- *Outcome:* Significant improvement in **sleep quality** (SMD pooled); GAD improved at 2 and 4 wk on HAM-A; only one RCT in insomnia (NS on ISI).
- *Quality:* **B.**
- *Takeaway:* Modest sleep-quality signal; apigenin is the proposed GABA-A active flavonoid.

**10.2 Zick SM, Wright BD, Sen A, Arnedt JT (2011).** *Preliminary examination of the efficacy and safety of a standardized chamomile extract for chronic primary insomnia: a randomized placebo-controlled pilot study.* **BMC Complement Altern Med 11:78.** PMID: 21939549. PMCID: PMC3198755.
- *Design / N:* RCT; n = 34 adults with chronic primary insomnia; 270 mg standardised extract bid × 28 days.
- *Outcome:* No significant difference vs placebo on primary endpoints (SOL, WASO); trends in daytime functioning.
- *Quality:* **B−.**
- *Takeaway:* Pleasant evening ritual (chamomile tea), but not a primary intervention.

## Circadian / Behavioural Hygiene — Highest ROI for Phase Advance

**Why this section matters:** Delayed sleep-wake phase is fundamentally a *circadian phase* problem, not a sedation problem. No diet or supplement except melatonin (prescription in UK) reliably shifts the dim-light melatonin onset (DLMO). Light timing and schedule consistency do.

**11.1 Sletten TL, Magee M, Murray JM, et al. (2018).** *Efficacy of melatonin with behavioural sleep-wake scheduling for delayed sleep-wake phase disorder: a double-blind, randomised clinical trial.* **PLoS Med 15(6):e1002587.** PMID: 29912983. DOI: 10.1371/journal.pmed.1002587.
- *Design / N:* RCT; n = 116 DSWPD patients; **0.5 mg melatonin** vs placebo, both arms with behavioural sleep-wake scheduling, dosed 1 h before desired bedtime.
- *Outcome:* Melatonin + behavioural scheduling advanced DLMO and sleep onset significantly more than placebo + scheduling; improved daytime function.
- *Quality:* **A.**
- *Takeaway:* In the UK, melatonin is Rx (Circadin / Slenyto / pharmacy specials). For this delayed sleep phase profile, **0.5 mg ~5–6 h before current sleep onset (e.g. ≈21:00 if onset is ~03:00), advancing by 30 min per 2–3 days as schedule shifts**, would be the formal protocol if a GP/private clinician prescribes it.

**11.2 Saxvig IW, Wilhelmsen-Langeland A, Pallesen S, Vedaa Ø, Nordhus IH, Bjorvatn B (2014).** *A randomized controlled trial with bright light and melatonin for delayed sleep phase disorder: effects on subjective and objective sleep.* **Chronobiol Int 31(1):72–86.** PMID: 24144243. DOI: 10.3109/07420528.2013.823200.
- *Design:* 2×2 RCT; bright light (morning 30 min) +/- melatonin in DSPD.
- *Outcome:* Combined treatment yielded the strongest phase advance and improved daytime alertness; light alone advanced sleep timing.
- *Quality:* **A−.**
- *Takeaway:* Pair morning light (10,000 lux box or daylight walk, ≥30 min within 15 min of waking) with progressively earlier wake-time anchored to ~07:00.

**11.3 Richardson C, Cain N, Bartel K, Micic G, Maddock B, Gradisar M (2018).** *A randomised controlled trial of bright light therapy and morning activity for adolescents and young adults with delayed sleep–wake phase disorder.* **Sleep Med 45:114–123.** PMID: 29680419. DOI: 10.1016/j.sleep.2018.02.001.
- *Design / N:* RCT in adolescents and young adults with DSWPD.
- *Outcome:* Morning bright light + structured morning activity advanced sleep timing meaningfully; effect smaller without compliance.
- *Quality:* **B+.**
- *Takeaway:* Behavioural anchoring (fixed wake time, getting outside immediately) is necessary even with light box use.

**11.4 Shechter A, Kim EW, St-Onge MP, Westwood AJ (2018).** *Blocking nocturnal blue light for insomnia: a randomized controlled trial.* **J Psychiatr Res 96:196–202.** PMID: 29101797. DOI: 10.1016/j.jpsychires.2017.10.015.
- *Design / N:* RCT crossover, n = 14 insomniacs; amber-tinted glasses 2 h pre-bed × 7 nights vs clear.
- *Outcome:* ~30 min ↑ subjective sleep duration; ↓ insomnia severity.
- *Quality:* **B.**
- *Takeaway:* Amber/blue-blocker glasses 2 h pre-bed, plus phone "night shift" / f.lux, are low-cost adjuncts.

## Practical Synthesis (delayed sleep phase, adult — phase-advance from ~3am to midnight target)

| Tier | Intervention | Source |
|------|--------------|--------|
| 1 — non-negotiable | **No caffeine after 14:00** (cut-off 6+ h pre-bed) | Drake 2013 |
| 1 | **Morning light ≥30 min within 15 min of waking** (anchor wake to fixed time, e.g. 07:00) | Saxvig 2014, Richardson 2018 |
| 1 | **Consistent wake time 7 days/week**; no >1 h drift | DSWPD consensus |
| 1 | **No alcohol within 4 h of bed** | He 2024 SR/MA |
| 2 — strong supplement | **Glycine 3 g, 30 min pre-bed** | Yamadera 2007, Bannai 2012 |
| 2 | **Magnesium glycinate 200–400 mg elemental, evening** | Abbasi 2012, Mah 2021 |
| 2 | **Dinner ~4 h pre-bed, high-GI carb (e.g. white rice)** | Afaghi 2007 |
| 3 — adjuncts | Tart-cherry concentrate 30 mL bid, or 2 kiwifruits 1 h pre-bed | Howatson 2012, Lin 2011 |
| 3 | Amber/blue-blocker glasses + phone night-mode 2 h pre-bed | Shechter 2018 |
| 3 | L-Tryptophan 1–3 g pre-bed (if no SSRI) | Sutanto 2022 |
| Rx (UK) | **Melatonin 0.5 mg ~5–6 h before current onset, advancing schedule** — see GP | Sletten 2018 |

**Expected combined effect (rough, based on pooled effect sizes):** SOL reduction ~25–40 min from supplement stack; circadian phase advance of 1–2 h/week from behavioural light/schedule protocol. Target a 30-min/day shift to avoid rebound.

## Quality Grading Key
- **A** = ≥1 large RCT or high-quality SR/MA, low risk of bias.
- **B** = small RCT(s) or moderate-quality SR; consistent direction.
- **C** = pilot, observational, or high heterogeneity.
