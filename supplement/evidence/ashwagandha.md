# Ashwagandha (Withania somnifera) — Evidence Review

**Patient context:** 29M Korean male, stress, hyperventilation, late sleep onset (~3am), fatigue. Considering KSM-66 ashwagandha.

---

## Summary

### (a) What KSM-66 does
KSM-66 is a full-spectrum, root-only ashwagandha extract standardized to ≥5% withanolides, developed by Ixoreal Biomed and used in most of the higher-quality stress/sleep RCTs since 2012. Pharmacologically, it appears to act primarily as an **HPA-axis modulator**: chronic dosing reduces morning serum cortisol by 14–28% versus placebo in stressed adults (Chandrasekhar 2012; Salve 2019; Lopresti 2019 [Shoden, comparator]). Proposed mechanisms include GABA-A receptor potentiation by withanolides (giving its mild anxiolytic and sleep-onset–shortening profile), down-regulation of NF-κB / TNF-α inflammatory signaling, mild thyrotropic effect (lowers TSH, raises T3/T4), and modest androgenic/anabolic effects in men (Wankhede 2015). Net effect: less "wired-but-tired," lower perceived stress, shorter sleep onset latency, better PSQI, modest testosterone and strength gains in resistance-trained men.

### (b) Who should take it
- Adults with chronic perceived stress (PSS ≥ 14–20), anxious-but-functional profile
- Late sleep-onset / "racing mind" insomnia with normal sleep architecture (good fit for the patient's 3am onset)
- Subclinical hypothyroidism (TSH 4.5–10) with euthyroid T4
- Resistance-training men wanting modest strength/recovery gains
- Elevated morning cortisol or burnout-pattern fatigue

### (c) Who should NOT take it
- **Pregnancy / breastfeeding** (classified abortifacient in Ayurvedic texts; insufficient safety data)
- **Hyperthyroidism / Graves'** — ashwagandha lowers TSH and raises T4 (Sharma 2018)
- **Autoimmune disease** (Hashimoto's, lupus, RA, MS) — immunostimulant effects may flare disease
- **Active liver disease, recent DILI, or chronic ALT elevation** — 23+ published cases of cholestatic hepatitis (LiverTox Likelihood C; TGA Australia safety alert Feb 2024; MHRA Yellow Card reports)
- Concurrent **immunosuppressants, sedatives/benzodiazepines, thyroid hormone replacement, anti-diabetics, antihypertensives** (additive effects)
- Scheduled surgery within 2 weeks (sedative interaction with anesthesia)

### (d) Dosing protocol
- **Standard:** 300 mg KSM-66 twice daily (600 mg/day) with meals, morning + evening
- **Lower-dose entry:** 300 mg once daily × 1 week, then titrate
- **Sleep-focused:** 300 mg with dinner, optionally 300 mg 1h pre-bed
- **Trial length:** 8–12 weeks. Most RCTs show effect at 4 weeks, plateau ~8 weeks. Re-evaluate at 12 weeks.
- **Cycle:** consider 8 weeks on / 2 weeks off; baseline LFTs (ALT/AST) and TSH before starting, repeat at 8 weeks if symptomatic
- **Stop and seek care if:** jaundice, RUQ pain, dark urine, pruritus, unusual fatigue, or new palpitations/heat intolerance

### (e) KSM-66 vs Sensoril vs generic
| Extract | Plant part | Withanolides | Best evidence for | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **KSM-66** | Root only | ≥5% | Stress, cortisol, sleep, strength, testosterone | Most-studied branded extract (Chandrasekhar, Salve, Langade, Wankhede); milder, daytime-suitable |
| **Sensoril** | Root + leaf | ≥10% | Stress, fatigue (Auddy 2008) | Higher leaf-derived withanolide content; more sedating, lower dose (125–250 mg); some concern that leaf withaferin-A may drive hepatotoxicity signal |
| **Shoden** | Root + leaf | ≥35% | Cortisol, DHEA-S (Lopresti 2019) | Very high potency, 120–240 mg dose |
| **Generic** | Variable | Unstandardized | None | Heavy-metal contamination risk (lead in Indian Ayurvedic products); skip |

Brand matters because (i) clinical trial doses were specific to standardized extracts, (ii) withanolide content varies 10-fold across commercial powders, and (iii) most hepatotoxicity case reports involve mixed/unbranded formulations or leaf-containing extracts. **For this patient, KSM-66 600 mg/day is the most evidence-aligned choice.**

## Papers

### 1. Chandrasekhar et al. 2012 — KSM-66, cortisol, chronic stress *(landmark)*
- **Citation:** Chandrasekhar K, Kapoor J, Anishetty S. *Indian J Psychol Med.* 2012;34(3):255-262. DOI: 10.4103/0253-7176.106022. PMID: 23439798.
- **Design / N:** RCT, double-blind, placebo-controlled, 60 days. N=64 adults with chronic stress.
- **Intervention:** KSM-66 300 mg BID (600 mg/day) vs placebo.
- **Outcomes:** Serum cortisol −27.9% vs placebo +7.9% (p=0.0006). PSS −44% vs placebo −5.5% (p<0.0001). GHQ-28 and DASS all significantly improved. AEs mild and equal across groups.
- **Quality:** **A** (foundational KSM-66 RCT, replicated since).
- **Takeaway:** Single best citation for the "ashwagandha lowers cortisol ~28%" claim.

### 2. Auddy et al. 2008 — Sensoril, chronic stress
- **Citation:** Auddy B, Hazra J, Mitra A, Abedon B, Ghosal S. *JANA.* 2008;11(1):50-56.
- **Design / N:** RCT, double-blind, placebo-controlled, 60 days. N=98 chronically stressed adults across 4 arms.
- **Intervention:** Sensoril 125 mg QD, 125 mg BID, 250 mg BID vs placebo.
- **Outcomes:** Dose-dependent reduction in cortisol, CRP, pulse rate, BP; modal Hamilton Anxiety improvement at 250 mg BID. Cortisol −24.2% at high dose.
- **Quality:** **B** (small per-arm N; published in JANA not PubMed-indexed at the time).
- **Takeaway:** Foundational Sensoril RCT; supports a separate, leaf-containing extract.

### 3. Pratte et al. 2014 — Systematic review, anxiety
- **Citation:** Pratte MA, Nanavati KB, Young V, Morley CP. *J Altern Complement Med.* 2014;20(12):901-908. DOI: 10.1089/acm.2014.0177. PMID: 25405876.
- **Design / N:** Systematic review of 5 human trials (total N≈400) using ashwagandha for anxiety.
- **Findings:** All 5 trials showed significant improvement in anxiety scales (HAM-A, Beck) vs control; methodological heterogeneity noted. No serious AEs across trials.
- **Quality:** **B** (narrative SR, no meta-analysis).
- **Takeaway:** First systematic confirmation that ashwagandha beats placebo for clinical anxiety.

### 4. Lopresti et al. 2019 — Shoden, stress + cortisol + DHEA-S
- **Citation:** Lopresti AL, Smith SJ, Malvi H, Kodgule R. *Medicine (Baltimore).* 2019;98(37):e17186. DOI: 10.1097/MD.0000000000017186. PMID: 31517876.
- **Design / N:** RCT, double-blind, 60 days, N=60.
- **Intervention:** Shoden 240 mg QD (35% withanolide root+leaf) vs placebo.
- **Outcomes:** HAM-A −41% vs placebo (p=0.04); morning cortisol significantly reduced (p<0.001); DHEA-S reduced (p=0.004); testosterone increased in males (p=0.038). Zero AEs.
- **Quality:** **A** (cleanest mechanism paper to date).
- **Takeaway:** HPA-axis modulation is the dominant mechanism; uses Shoden, NOT KSM-66 — flag if interpreting as KSM-66 evidence.

### 5. Salve et al. 2019 — KSM-66 dose-finding (Cureus)
- **Citation:** Salve J, Pate S, Debnath K, Langade D. *Cureus.* 2019;11(12):e6466. DOI: 10.7759/cureus.6466. PMID: 32021735.
- **Design / N:** RCT, double-blind, 3-arm, 8 weeks, N=60.
- **Intervention:** KSM-66 125 mg BID (250 mg/day) vs 300 mg BID (600 mg/day) vs placebo.
- **Outcomes:** Both doses reduced PSS; 600 mg/day stronger (p<0.001). Cortisol reduced at 600 mg/day (p<0.0001). Sleep-quality scale significantly improved in both ashwagandha arms.
- **Quality:** **B** (small N per arm; Cureus venue).
- **Takeaway:** 600 mg/day > 250 mg/day for both stress and cortisol; supports the standard dose.

### 6. Langade et al. 2019 — KSM-66, insomnia + anxiety
- **Citation:** Langade D, Kanchi S, Salve J, Debnath K, Ambegaokar D. *Cureus.* 2019;11(9):e5797. DOI: 10.7759/cureus.5797. PMID: 31728244.
- **Design / N:** RCT, double-blind, 10 weeks, N=60 patients with insomnia and anxiety.
- **Intervention:** KSM-66 300 mg BID vs placebo; actigraphy used.
- **Outcomes:** Sleep onset latency reduced (29.0 vs 33.9 min, p=0.019); sleep efficiency 75.6→83.5% (p<0.05); PSQI and HAM-A both improved (p<0.05). Well tolerated.
- **Quality:** **B** (small N; Cureus).
- **Takeaway:** Best direct evidence for the patient's specific complaint (late sleep onset).

### 7. Langade et al. 2020 — Ashwagandha, sleep in healthy + insomnia (J Ethnopharmacol)
- **Citation:** Langade D, Thakare V, Kanchi S, Kelgane S. *J Ethnopharmacol.* 2021;264:113276. DOI: 10.1016/j.jep.2020.113276. PMID: 32818573.
- **Design / N:** RCT, parallel-group, 8 weeks, N=80 (40 healthy, 40 insomnia).
- **Intervention:** 600 mg/day ashwagandha root extract vs placebo.
- **Outcomes:** SOL p<0.0001, sleep efficiency p<0.0001, TST p=0.002, WASO p=0.04. Larger effect in insomnia subgroup. No SAEs.
- **Quality:** **B** (peer-reviewed journal but same authorship cluster).
- **Takeaway:** Confirms sleep effect generalizes beyond Cureus paper; healthy adults also benefit.

### 8. Deshpande et al. 2020 — Generic ashwagandha, healthy adults, sleep
- **Citation:** Deshpande A, Irani N, Balkrishnan R, Benny IR. *Sleep Med.* 2020;72:28-36. DOI: 10.1016/j.sleep.2020.03.012. PMID: 32540634.
- **Design / N:** RCT, double-blind, 6 weeks, N=150 healthy adults with non-restorative sleep.
- **Intervention:** 120 mg ashwagandha root extract QD vs placebo.
- **Outcomes:** Sleep efficiency +72% vs placebo +29% (p<0.0001); WHO-5 wellbeing index improved; no AEs.
- **Quality:** **B** (large N, but lower dose / non-KSM-66 branded extract).
- **Takeaway:** Effect holds at lower doses in healthy population.

### 9. Wankhede et al. 2015 — KSM-66, strength, testosterone, body composition
- **Citation:** Wankhede S, Langade D, Joshi K, Sinha SR, Bhattacharyya S. *J Int Soc Sports Nutr.* 2015;12:43. DOI: 10.1186/s12970-015-0104-9. PMID: 26609282.
- **Design / N:** RCT, double-blind, 8 weeks, N=57 untrained males age 18–50.
- **Intervention:** KSM-66 300 mg BID + resistance training vs placebo + RT.
- **Outcomes:** Bench-press 1RM +46.0 vs +26.4 kg (p=0.001); testosterone +96.2 vs +18.0 ng/dL (p=0.004); body fat −3.5% vs −1.5% (p=0.03); CK recovery improved (p=0.03).
- **Quality:** **B** (industry-funded; effect sizes large but plausible).
- **Takeaway:** Best citation for "ashwagandha modestly raises testosterone in men."

### 10. Sharma et al. 2018 — KSM-66, subclinical hypothyroidism
- **Citation:** Sharma AK, Basu I, Singh S. *J Altern Complement Med.* 2018;24(3):243-248. DOI: 10.1089/acm.2017.0183. PMID: 28829155. (Published online 2017, print 2018.)
- **Design / N:** RCT, double-blind, 8 weeks, N=50 subclinical hypothyroid.
- **Intervention:** Ashwagandha root extract 600 mg/day vs placebo.
- **Outcomes:** TSH normalized (treatment −17.4% vs placebo +2.0%, p<0.001); T3 +41.5% (p<0.001); T4 +19.6% (p<0.001).
- **Quality:** **B**.
- **Takeaway:** Establishes thyrotropic effect — therapeutic for subclinical hypothyroid, but a contraindication signal for euthyroid Graves' or already-treated hyperthyroid patients.

### 11. Akhgarjand et al. 2022 — Meta-analysis, anxiety + stress
- **Citation:** Akhgarjand C, Asoudeh F, Bagheri A, et al. *Phytother Res.* 2022;36(11):4115-4124. DOI: 10.1002/ptr.7598. PMID: 35975583.
- **Design / N:** Meta-analysis of 12 RCTs (N≈1,002).
- **Outcomes:** Anxiety SMD −1.55 (95% CI −2.37, −0.74); stress SMD −1.75 (−2.29, −1.22). Doses 240–1250 mg/day. High heterogeneity (I²≈90%).
- **Quality:** **A** (most rigorous meta-analysis to date; heterogeneity caveat).
- **Takeaway:** Best single citation for effect size on stress/anxiety in modern literature.

### 12. Philips et al. 2023 — Ashwagandha hepatotoxicity case series
- **Citation:** Philips CA, Valsan A, Theruvath AH, et al. *Hepatol Commun.* 2023;7(10):e0270. DOI: 10.1097/HC9.0000000000000270. PMID: 37756041.
- **Design / N:** Multicenter case series, India + literature review. 8 new cases + literature pool of ~15 prior.
- **Outcomes:** Cholestatic hepatitis onset 2–12 weeks after starting ashwagandha; ALT/AST often >10× ULN; 1 fatality from acute liver failure; most resolve on cessation. RUCAM scores probable–highly probable.
- **Quality:** **B** (case series; cannot establish incidence).
- **Takeaway:** Hepatotoxicity is real but rare (~1:10,000s estimated). Now reflected in NIH LiverTox (Likelihood C) and TGA Australia safety alert Feb 2024.

## Verification notes / uncertainty flags

- The Auddy 2008 paper was published in *JANA*, which is not PubMed-indexed; I could not retrieve a DOI through Semantic Scholar. Cortisol effect size −24.2% is widely cited but should be treated as B-grade evidence.
- Chandrasekhar 2012 reports cortisol Δ as −27.9% in treatment vs +7.9% placebo (between-group difference cited as ~−28%); some secondary sources quote "−30%" — the precise figure is −27.9%.
- Lopresti 2019 used **Shoden, not KSM-66** — frequently miscited as KSM-66 evidence. Listed here as mechanism evidence only.
- No KSM-66 RCT exists for trials longer than ~12 weeks; long-term safety beyond 3 months is inferred from open-label extensions and post-marketing surveillance, not RCTs.
- Sharma "2018" thyroid paper has an online 2017 / print 2018 split — both dates appear in citations.

## Bottom line for this patient

A 12-week trial of **KSM-66 300 mg BID with meals** is well-justified by the evidence for his stress + late sleep onset + fatigue profile. Get baseline ALT/AST + TSH first. Stop and reassess at week 12, or sooner with any jaundice/RUQ symptoms. If subclinical hypothyroid is incidentally found, the thyrotropic effect is a feature; if he has any autoimmune family history, do TPO antibodies first.
