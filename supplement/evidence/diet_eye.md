# Diet, Nutrition & Eye Fatigue / Digital Eye Strain / Dry Eye — Evidence Review

**Patient:** 29M Korean, UK, occasional blurred vision when fatigued, heavy screen use, already on lutein.

## Bottom line (2-sentence summary)

The only dietary interventions with replicated RCT evidence for digital eye strain symptoms are **lutein/zeaxanthin** (improves contrast sensitivity, photostress recovery, and subjective eye strain in high screen users) and **astaxanthin** (improves accommodation amplitude and asthenopia in VDT workers); **omega-3** has mixed evidence — positive in short Indian computer-vision-syndrome RCTs but the large, high-quality DREAM trial was negative for established dry eye disease. Everything else (bilberry/anthocyanins, AREDS2 for non-AMD eyes, "eye vitamins" marketing blends, blue-light blocking glasses) is either weak evidence, indication-specific, or refuted; behavioural fixes (breaks, blink awareness, ergonomics, hydration) plausibly outperform supplements in dollar-for-symptom terms.

## 1. Stringham 2017 — Lutein + Zeaxanthin in high-screen-time adults (B.L.U.E. study)

- **Citation:** Stringham JM, Stringham NT, O'Brien KJ. *Macular Carotenoid Supplementation Improves Visual Performance, Sleep Quality, and Adverse Physical Symptoms in Those with High Screen Time Exposure.* Foods. 2017;6(7):47. doi:10.3390/foods6070047. PMID 28661438.
- **Design / N:** Double-blind RCT; 48 healthy young adults with ≥6 h/day screen time; 6 months.
- **Intervention:** 24 mg/day macular carotenoids (lutein + zeaxanthin + mesozeaxanthin) vs placebo.
- **Outcomes:** MPOD, contrast sensitivity, disability glare, photostress recovery, eye strain, fatigue, headache, sleep quality.
- **Effect:** Significant improvement in **all** visual performance measures and ~30% reduction in eye strain, eye fatigue, and headache frequency vs placebo (p<0.05 across measures).
- **Quality:** B (small N, industry-related funding, but pre-specified outcomes and double-blind).
- **Takeaway:** Strongest single RCT directly targeting the user's exact profile (young, high screen use). Justifies 10–20 mg lutein + 2–4 mg zeaxanthin daily.

## 2. Ma 2016 / Zhang 2021 — Lutein/zeaxanthin meta-analysis on MPOD and visual function

- **Citation:** Ma L, Liu R, Du JH, et al. *Lutein, Zeaxanthin and Meso-zeaxanthin Supplementation Associated with Macular Pigment Optical Density.* Nutrients. 2016;8(7):426. doi:10.3390/nu8070426.
- **Design / N:** Systematic review + meta-analysis; 20 RCTs, 938 AMD patients + 826 healthy subjects.
- **Effect:** Pooled MPOD increase WMD 0.07 (AMD) and 0.09 (healthy); contrast sensitivity improved at all four spatial frequencies vs placebo, with dose–response. Most consistent gains at 12 cycles/degree.
- **Quality:** A (meta-analysis of RCTs).
- **Takeaway:** Mechanism and outcome both replicated. Effects are real but modest; takes 3–6 months to manifest in MPOD.

## 3. Bhargava 2015 — Omega-3 for computer-vision-syndrome dry eye (large Indian RCT)

- **Citation:** Bhargava R, Kumar P, Phogat H, Kaur A, Kumar M. *Oral omega-3 fatty acids treatment in computer vision syndrome related dry eye.* Cont Lens Anterior Eye. 2015;38(3):206-10. doi:10.1016/j.clae.2015.01.007. PMID 25697893.
- **Design / N:** Multicentre, double-blind RCT; 478 symptomatic computer users (>3 h/day, ≥1 yr); 3 months.
- **Intervention:** EPA 360 mg + DHA 240 mg daily vs olive-oil placebo.
- **Outcomes / Effect:** Symptom score 8.0→3.9 (omega-3) vs 7.5→6.8 (placebo); TBUT 11.7→15.0 s vs 11.5→12.0 s; Schirmer modestly improved.
- **Quality:** B (large, double-blind, but single region, modest dose).
- **Takeaway:** In the specific niche of screen-driven dry eye (closest match to the user), omega-3 looks helpful. Note the dose is *small* (600 mg combined EPA+DHA) — supports food-first salmon/mackerel 2–3×/week rather than mega-doses.

## 4. DREAM Trial 2018 — Omega-3 for established dry eye (definitive negative)

- **Citation:** Asbell PA, Maguire MG, Pistilli M, et al. (Dry Eye Assessment and Management Study Research Group). *n-3 Fatty Acid Supplementation for the Treatment of Dry Eye Disease.* N Engl J Med. 2018;378(18):1681-1690. doi:10.1056/NEJMoa1709691. PMID 29652551.
- **Design / N:** Multicentre, double-blind RCT; 535 patients with moderate–severe dry eye disease; 12 months.
- **Intervention:** 3000 mg/day fish oil (2000 EPA + 1000 DHA) vs olive-oil placebo (2:1).
- **Outcomes / Effect:** OSDI change −13.9 (omega-3) vs −12.5 (placebo); between-group difference −1.9 (95% CI −5.0 to 1.1; p=0.21). No difference on TBUT, Schirmer, corneal/conjunctival staining.
- **Quality:** A (large, NIH-funded, gold-standard methodology).
- **Takeaway:** Largest, best-designed trial — **no benefit of high-dose fish-oil mono-therapy** for moderate–severe DED. Reconciles with #3: omega-3 may help mild screen-related symptoms or work synergistically with diet, but is not a treatment.

## 5. Nagaki 2002 / Nakamura 2004 — Astaxanthin for accommodation in VDT workers

- **Citations:**
  - Nagaki Y, Hayasaka S, Yamada T, et al. *Effects of astaxanthin on accommodation, critical flicker fusion, and pattern visual evoked potential in visual display terminal workers.* J Trad Med. 2002;19(5):170-173.
  - Nakamura A et al. *Changes in visual function following peroral astaxanthin.* Japanese J Clin Ophthalmol. 2004;58(6):1051-1054.
- **Design / N:** Small double-blind RCTs (n=13–40); 4 weeks.
- **Intervention:** Astaxanthin 4–12 mg/day vs placebo.
- **Outcomes / Effect:** ~46–54% reduction in self-reported eye fatigue; significantly improved positive accommodation time at 4 mg (p<0.05) and 12 mg (p<0.01); CFF and pattern-VEP unchanged.
- **Quality:** C (small N, Japanese-only, some industry involvement, replicated by ≥5 small trials with consistent direction).
- **Takeaway:** Most-replicated supplement for *accommodative* eye fatigue specifically. 4–6 mg/day is the practical dose; effect appears at 4 weeks.

## 6. AREDS2 2013 — Lutein/zeaxanthin + omega-3 for AMD (context only)

- **Citation:** AREDS2 Research Group. *Lutein + Zeaxanthin and Omega-3 Fatty Acids for Age-Related Macular Degeneration: The AREDS2 Randomized Clinical Trial.* JAMA. 2013;309(19):2005-2015. doi:10.1001/jama.2013.4997.
- **Design / N:** Multicentre RCT; 4,203 adults 50–85 with intermediate/advanced AMD; ~5 yrs.
- **Intervention arms:** Placebo / L10 + Z2 mg / DHA 350 + EPA 650 mg / both.
- **Effect on AMD progression:** L+Z HR 0.90 (p=0.12); DHA+EPA HR 0.97 (p=0.70); combined HR 0.89 (p=0.10). In low-dietary-L+Z subgroup, L+Z lowered progression risk.
- **Quality:** A (large, NIH-funded).
- **Takeaway:** AMD-specific and not applicable to a 29-year-old. Often misquoted by supplement brands. Use as a *dose anchor* (L 10 mg, Z 2 mg) — not as evidence the formula prevents anything in young eyes.

## 7. Walsh 2012 — Whole-body hydration and dry eye

- **Citation:** Walsh NP, Fortes MB, Raymond-Barker P, et al. *Is whole-body hydration an important consideration in dry eye?* Invest Ophthalmol Vis Sci. 2012;53(10):6622-7. doi:10.1167/iovs.12-10175. PMID 22952120.
- **Design / N:** Cross-sectional, 111 adults (mean age 77).
- **Effect:** Dry-eye subjects had higher plasma osmolality (Δ ~11 mOsm/kg, p=0.004, effect size d=0.83) — i.e., they were measurably under-hydrated. A 48-h rehydration protocol dropped both plasma and tear osmolarity.
- **Quality:** B (observational + small intervention, replicated mechanism).
- **Takeaway:** Drink water. Cheapest intervention in this entire document. Target pale-yellow urine, ~2 L/day for a 29M.

## 8. Singh / Cochrane 2023 — Blue-light filtering glasses (negative)

- **Citation:** Singh S, Downie LE, Anderson AJ. *Blue-light filtering spectacle lenses for visual performance, sleep, and macular health in adults.* Cochrane Database Syst Rev. 2023;8:CD013244. doi:10.1002/14651858.CD013244.pub2.
- **Design / N:** Cochrane review, 17 RCTs.
- **Effect:** Blue-light filtering lenses **probably make no difference** to short-term eye strain, sleep quality, or macular health vs standard lenses. Filters typically block 10–25% of blue light; daylight delivers ~1000× more blue light than a screen.
- **Quality:** A (Cochrane).
- **Takeaway:** Stop buying blue-blockers. Save the money for a proper desk lamp and a height-adjustable monitor.

## 9. Anthocyanins / bilberry — weakly supported

- **Key reviews:** Kalt W et al. *Recent research on polyphenolics in vision and eye health.* J Agric Food Chem. 2010;58(7):4001-4007 (PMID 20102149). Nomi Y, Iwasaki-Kurashige K, Matsumoto H. *Therapeutic Effects of Anthocyanins for Vision and Eye Health.* Molecules. 2019;24(18):3311 (PMID 31514422).
- **State of evidence:** In-vitro evidence that anthocyanins interact with rhodopsin and protect against oxidative retinal damage. Clinical RCTs are small (n<80), heterogeneous, mostly Japanese, with inconsistent symptom benefits.
- **Quality:** C.
- **Takeaway:** Eat blueberries/blackcurrants for general antioxidant intake — don't buy bilberry capsules expecting digital-eye-strain relief. (Note: a fighter-pilot WWII story is the origin myth; not real data.)

## 10. Vitamin A / carotenoids from food

Whole-food sources of pre-formed vitamin A (eggs, dairy) and provitamin-A carotenoids (sweet potato, carrots, spinach, kale, butternut squash) underpin rhodopsin function. **Frank vitamin A deficiency** causes night blindness and xerophthalmia, but this is essentially absent in UK diets. Lutein and zeaxanthin co-travel with dark leafy greens (spinach, kale) and egg yolks (high bioavailability). Recommendation: 1 serving leafy greens + 1 egg daily covers this without any pill.

## 11. 20-20-20 Rule — modest, requires adherence

- **Citation:** Talens-Estarelles C, Cerviño A, García-Lázaro S, et al. *The effects of breaks on digital eye strain, dry eye and binocular vision: Testing the 20-20-20 rule.* Cont Lens Anterior Eye. 2023;46(2):101744. doi:10.1016/j.clae.2022.101744. PMID 35963776.
- **Design / N:** Pre-post intervention, 29 symptomatic computer users.
- **Effect:** Symptom scores fell during active intervention; no change in objective tear-film/ocular surface measures; benefit did **not** persist after stopping.
- **Quality:** B–C (no parallel control, small N; supported by other small RCTs with daily reminders showing similar symptom reductions).
- **Takeaway:** Genuine but requires consistent use; an app/timer is the practical lever.

## Behavioural section — higher ROI than supplements

These are the levers that out-perform any capsule for digital eye strain. References given.

| Intervention | Evidence | Practical instruction |
|---|---|---|
| **20-20-20 rule + reminders** | Talens-Estarelles 2023 (PMID 35963776); Impact of 20-20-20 RCT 2024 (IJSHR) | Every 20 min look 20 ft (6 m) away for 20 s. Use a timer or browser extension. |
| **Blink awareness / blink training** | Nosch DS et al. PMID 34571605; npj Digital Medicine 2025 smartphone blink-training trial | Conscious complete blinks every few minutes; software/app-prompted blinking reduces TBUT-defined dryness over 4 weeks. |
| **Monitor distance & angle** | American Optometric Association CVS guidelines; AAO Ophthalmology 2022 review (PMID via S0161-6420(22)00361-X) | Screen 50–70 cm from eyes, top of screen at or just below eye level (so gaze is slightly downward — reduces exposed corneal surface). |
| **Ambient lighting & glare** | AAO CVS guideline | Avoid screen brighter than surroundings; eliminate reflective glare; reduce screen brightness in dim rooms. |
| **Humidity / airflow** | TFOS DEWS II Management Report 2017 | Avoid direct AC/heating airflow on face; office humidity >30%; humidifier in winter. |
| **Hydration** | Walsh 2012 (PMID 22952120) | ~2 L water/day; reduce alcohol and high-caffeine evenings. |
| **Blue-light glasses** | Cochrane 2023 (CD013244) | Skip — no evidence of benefit. |
| **Sleep & refraction check** | Standard ophth. practice | Get a current refraction; uncorrected mild astigmatism or +0.50 hyperopia is a common hidden cause of screen fatigue. Rule this out before supplementing. |

## Summary recommendation for this patient

1. **Keep the lutein** (strongest evidence, matches profile); 10–20 mg lutein + 2 mg zeaxanthin daily with a fatty meal. Add astaxanthin 4–6 mg/day if accommodation-type fatigue (focusing strain) is dominant — 4-week trial. Evidence grade B.
2. **Food first for omega-3:** oily fish 2–3×/week (salmon, mackerel, sardines). Skip the 3 g fish-oil capsules — DREAM was negative.
3. **Hydration + leafy greens + eggs daily** — cheap, no downside.
4. **Behavioural fixes first**: get a refraction check, fix monitor ergonomics, install a 20-20-20 timer, practise conscious blinking, manage room humidity. These plausibly beat any supplement.
5. **Skip:** blue-light blocking glasses, bilberry pills, AREDS2 formulas (designed for elderly AMD patients, not 29-year-olds).
