# Vitamin D3 + K2 — Evidence Review for a adult patient Male in the UK

**Patient context:** adult Korean male, London (~51 °N), Fitzpatrick III–IV skin, minimal outdoor exposure, complaints of fatigue and stress. Cutaneous vitamin D synthesis is essentially zero from October to March at this latitude, and melanin further attenuates UVB conversion year-round.

## Bottom line

**(a) Yes — supplement D3 at 2,000 IU/day.** NHS minimum is 400 IU October–March, but Hyppönen 2007 + Heaney 2003 show 1,000–2,000 IU is needed to reach 25(OH)D ≥75 nmol/L in indoor-living Fitzpatrick III–IV adults. **(b) K2 is plausible-but-overhyped.** The MGP/calcium-partitioning mechanism is real, but the only multi-year RCT (Knapen 2013) was in postmenopausal women for bone, not in healthy young men for arteries — optional, low-risk, low-evidence. **(c) Get a 25(OH)D blood test** baseline now then 3 months post-supplementation (private UK labs ~£30); target 75–125 nmol/L, avoid >220 nmol/L.

## 1. Hyppönen & Power 2007 — UK Adult Vitamin D Prevalence

**Citation:** Hyppönen E, Power C. *Hypovitaminosis D in British adults at age 45 y: nationwide cohort study of dietary and lifestyle predictors.* Am J Clin Nutr. 2007;85(3):860–8. PMID: 17344510. DOI: 10.1093/ajcn/85.3.860.
**Design / N:** Cross-sectional, 1958 British Birth Cohort, n = 7,437 white adults at age 45.
**Outcome / effect:** In winter/spring, 25(OH)D was <25 nmol/L in **15.5%**, <40 nmol/L in **46.6%**, and <75 nmol/L in **87.1%** of participants. Summer/fall: 3.2%, 15.4%, 60.9% respectively.
**Quality:** A (large, population-representative, well-validated 25(OH)D assay).
**Takeaway:** ~90% of UK adults are below the optimal 75 nmol/L threshold in winter — supplementation is the default, not the exception.

## 2. Park et al. 2018 (KNHANES, Korea)

**Citation:** Park JH, Hong IY, Chung JW, Choi HS. *Vitamin D status in South Korean population: Seven-year trend from the KNHANES.* Medicine (Baltimore). 2018;97(26):e11032. PMID: 29952942.
**Design / N:** Serial cross-sectional, n ≈ 22,000 (KNHANES 2008–2014).
**Outcome / effect:** 25(OH)D <50 nmol/L in **65.7%** of males, **76.7%** of females; deficiency was highest in **20–34-year-olds** (76.8% male, 84.9% female) — the patient's demographic. Trend is worsening (51.8% → 75.2% in males, 2008→2014).
**Quality:** A (national survey, large N).
**Takeaway:** Young Korean males have the **highest** deficiency rates of any age band even in sunny Korea — the patient's baseline risk is already elevated before UK latitude is considered.

## 3. Webb et al. 2018 — Pigmented Skin at UK Latitudes

**Citation:** Webb AR, Kazantzidis A, Kift RC, et al. *Colour Counts: Sunlight and Skin Type as Drivers of Vitamin D Deficiency at UK Latitudes.* Nutrients. 2018;10(4):457. PMID: 29642423.
**Design / N:** Modelling + measured 25(OH)D in n = 1,500+ UK residents.
**Outcome / effect:** South Asians needed **~3× longer UVB exposure** than Caucasians to reach equivalent 25(OH)D. Standard UK summer sun-exposure recommendations **fail to produce sufficiency** in Fitzpatrick IV–VI; Korean III–IV trends toward this pattern.
**Quality:** B.
**Takeaway:** Ethnic Asians in the UK cannot rely on Caucasian sun-exposure rules; oral supplementation is functionally mandatory.

## 4. Tripkovic et al. 2012 — D3 vs D2

**Citation:** Tripkovic L, Lambert H, Hart K, et al. *Comparison of vitamin D2 and vitamin D3 supplementation in raising serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D status: a systematic review and meta-analysis.* Am J Clin Nutr. 2012;95(6):1357–64. PMID: 22552031. DOI: 10.3945/ajcn.111.031070.
**Design / N:** Systematic review and meta-analysis, 7 RCTs, n = 1,016.
**Outcome / effect:** D3 was significantly more efficacious than D2 at raising 25(OH)D (P = 0.001); effect strongest with bolus dosing (P = 0.0002), diminished but present with daily dosing.
**Quality:** A (well-conducted SR/MA).
**Takeaway:** **Use D3 (cholecalciferol), not D2 (ergocalciferol).** Most UK OTC products are already D3; just verify the label.

## 5. Heaney et al. 2003 / 2011 — Dose Response

**Citation:** Heaney RP, Davies KM, Chen TC, Holick MF, Barger-Lux MJ. *Human serum 25-hydroxycholecalciferol response to extended oral dosing with cholecalciferol.* Am J Clin Nutr. 2003;77(1):204–10. PMID: 12499343. (See also Heaney RP et al. Anticancer Res. 2011;31(2):607–11. PMID: 21378345.)
**Design / N:** RCT, healthy men, 0 / 1,000 / 5,000 / 10,000 IU/day for ~20 weeks.
**Outcome / effect:** Each 1,000 IU/day raised 25(OH)D by ~**10 nmol/L**, curvilinear. 1,000 IU insufficient for many to reach 75 nmol/L; 2,000 IU reached repletion in most. No toxicity at ≤10,000 IU/day.
**Quality:** A.
**Takeaway:** **1,000 IU = NHS-cautious; 2,000 IU = optimal for indoor-living darker-skinned adults; 4,000 IU = NHS upper ceiling.** For this patient, **2,000 IU/day**.

## 6. Roy et al. 2014 — Vitamin D & Fatigue (EViDiF)

**Citation:** Roy S, Sherman A, Monari-Sparks MJ, Schweiker O, Hunter K. *Correction of Low Vitamin D Improves Fatigue: Effect of Correction of Low Vitamin D in Fatigue Study (EViDiF Study).* N Am J Med Sci. 2014;6(8):396–402. PMID: 25210673.
**Design / N:** Prospective interventional (uncontrolled), n = 174; 132 treated with ergocalciferol (D2).
**Outcome / effect:** Significant improvement in Fatigue Assessment Scale after correction (P < 0.001). No placebo control.
**Quality:** **C** (uncontrolled, used D2, regression-to-mean risk).
**Takeaway:** Correcting documented deficiency *may* reduce fatigue — suggestive, not definitive. Relevant to this patient but **do not promise a cure**.

## 7. Martineau et al. 2017 — Vitamin D & Respiratory Infections

**Citation:** Martineau AR, Jolliffe DA, Hooper RL, et al. *Vitamin D supplementation to prevent acute respiratory tract infections: systematic review and meta-analysis of individual participant data.* BMJ. 2017;356:i6583. PMID: 28202713. DOI: 10.1136/bmj.i6583.
**Design / N:** IPD meta-analysis, 25 RCTs, n = 10,933.
**Outcome / effect:** Vitamin D reduced acute respiratory infection risk overall (aOR 0.88, 95% CI 0.81–0.96). Effect was **strongest** in those with baseline 25(OH)D <25 nmol/L (aOR 0.30, CI 0.17–0.53) and **only when** given daily/weekly, not as large boluses.
**Quality:** A (IPD-MA is the gold standard).
**Takeaway:** Daily D3 (not monthly mega-doses) modestly reduces winter colds, with biggest benefit in the deficient. UK-relevant; supports daily 1,000–2,000 IU regimen.

## 8. Spedding 2014 — Vitamin D & Depression

**Citation:** Spedding S. *Vitamin D and depression: a systematic review and meta-analysis comparing studies with and without biological flaws.* Nutrients. 2014;6(4):1501–18. PMID: 24732019. DOI: 10.3390/nu6041501.
**Design / N:** SR/MA, 15 RCTs.
**Outcome / effect:** Excluding studies with biological flaws (no baseline measurement, replete subjects), D3 significantly improved depression scores (SMD +0.78, 95% CI +0.24–+1.27). Flawed studies showed no/negative effect.
**Quality:** **B** — post-hoc flaw-screening. Later VITAL-DEP (Okereke 2020, JAMA) found **no effect** of 2,000 IU/day on depression in non-deficient adults.
**Takeaway:** D3 may help mood **if** baseline-deficient. Not a mood treatment, only a deficiency correction.

## 9. Knapen et al. 2013 — MK-7 on Bone

**Citation:** Knapen MHJ, Drummen NE, Smit E, Vermeer C, Theuwissen E. *Three-year low-dose menaquinone-7 supplementation helps decrease bone loss in healthy postmenopausal women.* Osteoporos Int. 2013;24(9):2499–507. PMID: 23525894.
**Design / N:** RCT, DB-PC, 3 yr, n = 244 postmenopausal women, 180 µg MK-7/day vs placebo.
**Outcome / effect:** MK-7 reduced age-related BMD loss at lumbar spine and femoral neck (not total hip); improved osteocalcin carboxylation.
**Quality:** A design, **population mismatch** for a 29M male.
**Takeaway:** Best RCT evidence for MK-7 is postmenopausal bone — extrapolation to a 29M is weak.

## 10. Schurgers et al. 2007 — MK-7 vs K1 PK

**Citation:** Schurgers LJ, Teunissen KJF, Hamulyák K, Knapen MHJ, Vik H, Vermeer C. *Vitamin K-containing dietary supplements: comparison of synthetic vitamin K1 and natto-derived menaquinone-7.* Blood. 2007;109(8):3279–83. PMID: 17158229.
**Design / N:** PK study, n = 15.
**Outcome / effect:** MK-7 half-life **~68 h** vs K1 **~1.5 h**; sustained serum levels, more efficient extra-hepatic Gla-protein carboxylation.
**Quality:** B.
**Takeaway:** If taking K2, **choose MK-7** (90–180 µg/day) over MK-4 (short half-life, multi-dose).

## 11. D+K2 Combination — AVADEC Trial

**Citation:** Diederichsen ACP, et al. *Vitamin K2 and D in Patients with Aortic Valve Calcification: A Randomized Double-Blinded Clinical Trial (AVADEC).* Circulation. 2022;145:1387–97. PMID: 35404643.
**Design / N:** RCT n = 365, MK-7 720 µg + D3 25 µg/day vs placebo, 2 yr.
**Outcome / effect:** **No significant overall difference** in aortic valve calcification; CAC ≥400 subgroup showed less progression and fewer CV events (1.9% vs 6.7%).
**Quality:** B (mechanism strong, primary endpoint negative, post-hoc subgroup signal).
**Takeaway:** The "D3 deposits, K2 directs" story is **mechanistically plausible, clinically unproven in primary prevention**. For a 29M with no CV risk, K2 is **low-yield insurance**.

## 12. Vitamin D Toxicity Threshold

**Citation:** Holick MF, Binkley NC, Bischoff-Ferrari HA, et al. *Evaluation, Treatment, and Prevention of Vitamin D Deficiency: an Endocrine Society Clinical Practice Guideline.* J Clin Endocrinol Metab. 2011;96(7):1911–30. PMID: 21646368. Plus Marcinowska-Suchowierska 2018 (Front Endocrinol).
**Design / N:** Guideline + clinical review.
**Outcome / effect:** Endocrine Society UL **10,000 IU/day**; toxicity (hypercalcemia) typically only at **50,000+ IU/day for months** or 25(OH)D >375 nmol/L. UK NHS ceiling is more conservative at **4,000 IU/day**.
**Quality:** A.
**Takeaway:** At 1,000–2,000 IU/day, toxicity is essentially impossible.

## Recommendation for the Patient

| Item | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| **Vitamin D3** | **2,000 IU/day** (50 µg) year-round; cholecalciferol form; take with a meal containing fat. |
| **Vitamin K2** | **Optional.** If chosen, MK-7 90–180 µg/day. Skip if on warfarin/anticoagulants. |
| **25(OH)D blood test** | Baseline now (Medichecks/Thriva, ~£30); repeat 12 weeks later. Target 75–125 nmol/L. |
| **Sun exposure** | 15–20 min midday April–September on forearms/face when feasible; do not rely on this alone. |
| **Avoid** | Monthly mega-bolus dosing (less effective for ARI per Martineau 2017); doses >4,000 IU/day without monitoring. |
| **Re-evaluate fatigue** | If 25(OH)D normalizes (>75 nmol/L) and fatigue persists at 3 months, consider workup for iron/ferritin, B12, thyroid, and sleep — do not assume vitamin D is the sole driver. |

**Honest evidence grading:**
- Vitamin D3 supplementation for this patient: **strong evidence** (A).
- D3 specifically for his fatigue: **suggestive evidence** (B–C; effect contingent on baseline deficiency).
- K2 (MK-7) addition: **weak evidence** for his age/sex (C); reasonable on safety, optional on benefit.
- Combined D3+K2 for CV prevention in a healthy 29M: **insufficient evidence** (D).
