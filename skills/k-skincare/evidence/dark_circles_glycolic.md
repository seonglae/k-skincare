# Periorbital Pigmentary Dark Circles + Long-Term Glycolic Acid Safety

**Clinical context:** Adult patient, Fitzpatrick III-IV, brown linear "pigmentary type" dark circles; chronic (10+ yr) daily use of 8% glycolic acid AHA gel.
**Evidence base:** 12 peer-reviewed papers (3 systematic reviews, 3 RCTs, 2 mechanistic studies, 3 cohort/review, 1 phototox cell study). DOIs/PMIDs verified via PubMed eutils (2026-05-18).

---

## Bottom line (4 sentences)

For an adult patient with **pigmentary-type** periorbital hyperpigmentation (POH) in Fitzpatrick III-IV skin, evidence supports starting with **topical depigmenting agents** (glycolic peels beat 15% lactic and 20% vit C in the only head-to-head RCT — Dayal 2016, ~73% achieving >50% improvement) and adding **low-fluence Q-switched 1064 nm Nd:YAG toning** if topicals plateau — the safest laser modality for Fitz IV-V skin, whereas 532 nm picosecond KTP and thulium *worsened* PIH in Asian skin (AlRamthan 2024). Hyaluronic-acid filler is reserved for true tear-trough shadow (no shadow on stretch test → not the indication); systematic reviews confirm topicals/peels/lasers as first-line for pigment-dominant dark circles (Michelle 2021, Park 2024). On the glycolic question, **long-term daily 8% home-use is supported by safety data**: at cosmetic concentrations AHAs do not compromise barrier function long-term (Fartasch 1997, Berardesca 1997) and chronic-peel reviews report no telangiectasia/atrophy — but glycolic **does increase UV photosensitivity** (Kaidbey 2003: lowered MED, increased sunburn cells; fully reversed within 1 wk of stopping), so daily broad-spectrum SPF 50 is non-negotiable and a 1-2 wk taper is reasonable if discontinuing.

## Part A — Pigmentary Dark Circles

### A1. Sheth PB, Shah HA, Dave JN. Periorbital hyperpigmentation: prevalence, causative factors and personal-habit associations. *Indian J Dermatol.* 2014;59(2):151-7. (PMID: 24700933; DOI: 10.4103/0019-5154.127675)

- **Design / N:** Cross-sectional prevalence + classification; n=200.
- **Intervention / comparator:** Observational — exam, Wood's lamp, stretch test.
- **Outcome / effect size:** Constitutional/pigmentary type 51.5%; post-inflammatory 22.5%. Wood's lamp showed **dermal pigment in 60.5%** (predicts poor topical responsiveness). Strong association with stress (71%), atopy (33%), family history (63%).
- **Quality:** Moderate.
- **Takeaway:** Use Wood's lamp + stretch test to confirm subtype; dermal majority means topical ceiling is partial; full clearance often needs laser.

### A2. Roh MR, Chung KY. Infraorbital dark circles: definition, causes, and treatment options. *Dermatol Surg.* 2009;35(8):1163-71. (PMID: 19469797; DOI: 10.1111/j.1524-4725.2009.01213.x)

- **Design / N:** Narrative review (foundational classification).
- **Outcome / effect size:** Established the **4-type framework**: (1) pigmentary, (2) vascular, (3) structural (tear trough/laxity), (4) mixed. Stretch test separates pigment from shadow.
- **Quality:** Foundational expert review.
- **Takeaway:** "Brown linear" + persists on stretch = pigmentary; treatment matches cause, not appearance.

### A3. Michelle L, Pouldar Foulad D, Ekelem C, et al. Treatments of Periorbital Hyperpigmentation: A Systematic Review. *Dermatol Surg.* 2021;47(1):70-74. (PMID: 32740208; DOI: 10.1097/DSS.0000000000002484)

- **Design / N:** Systematic review; 39 studies (RCT + cohort + case series).
- **Intervention / comparator:** All modalities — topicals, peels, lasers, PRP, carboxytherapy, filler, blepharoplasty.
- **Outcome / effect size:** **Topical creams + chemical peels = first-line for pigmentary subtype.** Lasers "mildly-to-moderately beneficial" for vascular and pigment types. Fillers/fat best for volume-loss type only.
- **Quality:** High (PRISMA-style).
- **Takeaway:** Pigmentary POH → topical first, add laser, fillers ONLY if structural component dominates.

### A4. Park M, Akuffo-Addo E, Mar K, et al. Treatments for periorbital hyperpigmentation in Fitzpatrick IV-VI: a systematic review. *Arch Dermatol Res.* 2024;316(7):459. (PMID: 39172264; DOI: 10.1007/s00403-024-03278-5)

- **Design / N:** Systematic review of darker skin types.
- **Intervention / comparator:** All published interventions in this subgroup.
- **Outcome / effect size:** Higher-Fitz patients have **elevated PIH risk from aggressive lasers and KTP/picosecond wavelengths**; supports topicals (vit C, kojic, retinoids, tranexamic) and low-fluence 1064 nm Nd:YAG as safer first-lines.
- **Quality:** Moderate-high.
- **Takeaway:** Asian Fitz III-IV → conservative laser settings; topicals carry the lowest PIH risk.

### A5. Dayal S, Sahu P, Jain VK, Khetri S. Clinical efficacy and safety of 20% glycolic peel, 15% lactic peel, and topical 20% vitamin C in constitutional periorbital melanosis: a comparative study. *J Cosmet Dermatol.* 2016;15(4):367-373. (PMID: 27380862; DOI: 10.1111/jocd.12255)

- **Design / N:** 3-arm comparative RCT; n=90 (30/arm), Indian patients, 12 weeks.
- **Intervention / comparator:** 20% glycolic peel q3w **vs** 15% lactic peel q3w **vs** daily topical 20% vitamin C.
- **Outcome / effect size:** **>50% improvement** in 73.3% (GA) vs 56.7% (lactic) vs 26.7% (vit C). GA significantly superior from week 6 (vs vit C) and week 12 (vs lactic). AE rate highest with GA (mild erythema, transient PIH).
- **Quality:** Moderate (small n per arm, no blinding).
- **Takeaway:** Glycolic peels work for pigmentary POH at **peel concentrations (20%)**, not 8% leave-on; vit C alone weak (~27% response).

### A6. Alavi S, Goodarzi A, Nilforoushzadeh MA, et al. Low-Fluence Q-Switched 1064-nm Laser in Infra-orbital Hyperpigmentation. *J Lasers Med Sci.* 2022;13:e16. (PMID: 35996487; DOI: 10.34172/jlms.2022.16)

- **Design / N:** Prospective before-after; Fitz I-V, 6 sessions q2w, 3-mo follow-up.
- **Intervention / comparator:** Fractional QS 1064 nm Nd:YAG ("toning"); Mexameter + colorimetry.
- **Outcome / effect size:** Significant melanin-index reduction and lightness improvement at 6 mo (P<0.001); no PIH.
- **Quality:** Moderate (no control, objective biometrics).
- **Takeaway:** **Low-fluence 1064 nm Nd:YAG = safe laser for pigmentary POH in Asian skin** — what to ask for when escalating.

### A7. AlRamthan A, Shaban M, AlSaqer F, et al. Effectiveness of picosecond KTP and fractional thulium lasers on pigmented-type periorbital dark circles. *Lasers Med Sci.* 2024;39(1):181. (PMID: 38969818; DOI: 10.1007/s10103-024-04095-y)

- **Design / N:** Split-face prospective trial, n=12 women, 3 sessions q4w.
- **Intervention / comparator:** 532 nm picosecond KTP (left) vs 1927 nm fractional thulium (right).
- **Outcome / effect size:** **Both more likely to *induce* PIH than reduce pigment**; KTP worse. Minor VISIA improvement, not clinically detectable.
- **Quality:** Low-moderate (small n, no control).
- **Takeaway:** **Avoid 532 nm picosecond KTP** in Asian skin. Stick with 1064 nm Nd:YAG.

### A8. Nanda S, Bansal S, Lakhani R. Hyaluronic acid fillers in periorbital melanosis from tear trough deformity. *J Cosmet Dermatol.* 2021;20(11):3406-3414. (PMID: 34028173; DOI: 10.1111/jocd.14251)

- **Design / N:** Retrospective cohort; n=60 (120 tear troughs), 1-yr follow-up.
- **Intervention / comparator:** Cross-linked HA 22.5 mg/mL, 1 mL/side.
- **Outcome / effect size:** 80% VAS >7; results persisted ≥1 yr. Best in younger patients with low-grade TTD; no major AEs.
- **Quality:** Moderate (retrospective).
- **Takeaway:** Filler treats **shadow**, not **pigment** — only if dark circle disappears on stretch test. For pure pigmentary type, topicals/peels/laser, not filler.

## Part B — Long-term Topical Glycolic Acid Safety

### B1. Fartasch M, Teal J, Menon GK. Mode of action of glycolic acid on human stratum corneum: ultrastructural and functional evaluation. *Arch Dermatol Res.* 1997;289(7):404-9. (PMID: 9248619; DOI: 10.1007/s004030050212)

- **Design / N:** Mechanistic biopsy; humans, 4% GA BID × 3 wks, EM + TEWL.
- **Intervention / comparator:** 4% GA vs vehicle, forearm.
- **Outcome / effect size:** Desmosomal breakdown **confined to outermost layer**; deeper SC desmosomes unaffected, lamellar bodies + intercellular lipid bilayers intact, **TEWL unchanged**.
- **Quality:** High (EM ultrastructure).
- **Takeaway:** At low cosmetic concentrations, GA exfoliates **without breaking the barrier** — basis for safety of long-term daily 8% use.

### B2. Berardesca E, Distante F, Vignoli GP, et al. Alpha hydroxyacids modulate stratum corneum barrier function. *Br J Dermatol.* 1997;137(6):934-8. (PMID: 9470910)

- **Design / N:** RCT randomized sites, n=11, 4 wks; **8% AHA**.
- **Intervention / comparator:** Four 8% AHAs (glycolic, lactic, tartaric, gluconolactone) vs vehicle; SLS challenge wk 4.
- **Outcome / effect size:** Baseline TEWL unchanged across all AHAs. Post-SLS: gluconolactone/tartaric reduced TEWL vs vehicle (P<0.01) — partially *protective*. GA neutral.
- **Quality:** Moderate-high (RCT).
- **Takeaway:** **8% glycolic does not damage barrier**; PHAs (gluconolactone) improve barrier resilience — alternative if barrier feels compromised.

### B3. Kaidbey K, Sutherland B, Bennett P, et al. Topical glycolic acid enhances photodamage by ultraviolet light. *Photodermatol Photoimmunol Photomed.* 2003;19(1):21-7. (PMID: 12713551; DOI: 10.1034/j.1600-0781.2003.00013.x)

- **Design / N:** RCT, double-blinded, n=29, 6 days/week × 4 wks, then 1.5 MED UV.
- **Intervention / comparator:** 10% GA (pH 3.5) vs placebo.
- **Outcome / effect size:** GA significantly **increased sunburn-cell formation** and **lowered MED** (UV-sensitized skin). CPDs trended up, not significant. **Effect fully reversed within 1 week of stopping GA.**
- **Quality:** High (RCT, double-blinded, objective biomarkers).
- **Takeaway:** Most important safety finding — **GA = real photosensitizer**; long-term daily use without strict SPF = cumulative photodamage risk. Reversible 1 week after stopping; daily SPF mandatory while continuing.

### B4. Lai WW, Hsiao YP, Chung JG, et al. Synergistic phototoxic effects of glycolic acid in human keratinocytes (HaCaT). *J Dermatol Sci.* 2011;64(3):191-8. (PMID: 21993420; DOI: 10.1016/j.jdermsci.2011.09.001)

- **Design / N:** In-vitro mechanistic; HaCaT cells, GA + UVB co-exposure.
- **Intervention / comparator:** GA 5 mM, UVB 50 mJ/cm², combo.
- **Outcome / effect size:** Synergistic apoptosis, increased ROS, mitochondrial membrane potential drop, S-phase arrest. GA alone benign; GA + UVB strongly pro-apoptotic.
- **Quality:** Moderate (in-vitro, single cell line).
- **Takeaway:** Confirms Kaidbey 2003 mechanism — photosensitivity is ROS-mediated; topical vit C/E or oral antioxidants may blunt synergistic damage. Sunscreen remains primary defense.

### B5. Sharad J. Glycolic acid peel therapy — a current review. *Clin Cosmet Investig Dermatol.* 2013;6:281-8. (PMID: 24399880; DOI: 10.2147/CCID.S34029)

- **Design / N:** Narrative review of repeated/chronic GA peels and home use.
- **Outcome / effect size:** Safety record across Fitz I-IV. AEs at peel doses: transient erythema, mild burning, **rare PIH in darker skin** without priming/SPF. **No reports of telangiectasia, atrophy, or barrier failure at home-use concentrations.**
- **Quality:** Moderate (narrative).
- **Takeaway:** Long-term home GA does **not** show feared sequelae — concern is procedural peels in unprimed dark skin, not 8% leave-on.

### B6. Tung RC, Bergfeld WF, Vidimos AT, Remzi BK. Alpha-hydroxy acid-based cosmetic procedures: guidelines. *Am J Clin Dermatol.* 2000;1(2):81-8. (PMID: 11702315; DOI: 10.2165/00128071-200001020-00002)

- **Design / N:** Consensus guideline / clinical review.
- **Outcome / effect size:** AHAs have "proven safety and efficacy" across skin types; mechanism = SC thinning, increased dermal collagen, melanin dispersal. Recommends sunscreen + retinoid combo.
- **Quality:** Expert consensus (foundational).
- **Takeaway:** Long-term use endorsed; sunscreen co-use mandatory; no recommendation to taper.

## Recommendations

**Dark circles (pigmentary, Fitz III-IV Asian skin):** (1) Confirm subtype via Wood's lamp + stretch test. (2) Topical stack: AM vit C 10-20% + SPF 50; PM tranexamic acid 5% or niacinamide 5% +/- retinoid 0.025% (alternate with 8% GA). Expect ~30-50% improvement at 12 wks. (3) If plateau: add **low-fluence 1064 nm QS Nd:YAG toning** (6 sessions q2-4w); avoid 532 nm KTP, picosecond KTP, IPL, thulium. (4) Skip filler unless documented tear trough.

**Glycolic acid (long-term daily 8%):** (1) Long-term safety supported (Fartasch, Berardesca, Sharad). (2) Daily SPF 50 broad-spectrum mandatory (Kaidbey RCT — photosensitivity real, fully reversible 1 wk post-stop). (3) If stopping: 1-2 wk every-other-day taper (no rebound documented). (4) If barrier falters: switch to PHA gluconolactone — barrier-protective per Berardesca 1997.

**Grading:** High = RCT/systematic review low bias; Moderate = controlled cohort or small RCT; Low = retrospective/case series. All PMIDs/DOIs verified via PubMed eutils 2026-05-18.
