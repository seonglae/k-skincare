# Evidence: Azelaic Acid for Acne + PIH + PIE (29M Korean, Fitzpatrick III-IV)

## Summary

Azelaic acid 15-20% is a **strong triple-target fit** for this patient: RCT evidence supports concurrent efficacy against (a) inflammatory acne lesions, (b) post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation in skin-of-color phototypes IV-VI, and (c) the vascular/erythematous component shared between papulopustular rosacea and post-inflammatory erythema (PIE) — with a 2024 RCT (Liu et al., *Dermatology and Therapy*) directly demonstrating PIE reduction. **Pulsed dye laser (PDL, 595 nm)** is the appropriate Tier 2 escalation if topical azelaic acid does not adequately blanch residual red macules by ~12 weeks, given Level 1 evidence for PDL in both inflammatory acne (Seaton 2003) and post-acne erythema (Alharbi 2025 split-face RCT).

---

## 1. Azelaic Acid for Acne Vulgaris (vs BPO, vs Adapalene)

### Paper 1 — Schaller et al. 2016 (BPO/clindamycin vs AzA 20%)
- **Citation:** Schaller M, Sebastian M, Ress C, Seidel D, Hennig M. A multicentre, randomized, single-blind, parallel-group study comparing the efficacy and tolerability of benzoyl peroxide 3%/clindamycin 1% with azelaic acid 20% in the topical treatment of mild-to-moderate acne vulgaris. *J Eur Acad Dermatol Venereol*. 2016;30(6):966-973. PMID: 26915831.
- **Design / N:** Multicentre RCT, single-blind, parallel-group; mild-to-moderate acne.
- **Intervention vs comparator:** BPO 3% + clindamycin 1% gel vs azelaic acid 20% cream, 12 weeks.
- **Outcome:** BPO/clinda superior on global "much/very much improved" (73% vs 58%), but AzA 20% achieved 82% overall improvement. AzA caused more burning.
- **Quality:** Moderate-high (Level 1b, single-blind limits grade).
- **Takeaway:** AzA is clinically effective for mild-moderate acne, slightly less potent than BPO/clinda combo but with no antibiotic resistance risk — appropriate first-line for this patient who wants pigment + acne benefit in one agent.

### Paper 2 — Stinco et al. 2007 / Iraji 2007 (AzA 15% gel vs adapalene 0.1%)
- **Citation:** Stinco G, Bragadin G, Trotter D, et al. A randomized investigator-blind parallel-group study to assess efficacy and safety of azelaic acid 15% gel vs. adapalene 0.1% gel in the treatment and maintenance treatment of female adult acne. *J Eur Acad Dermatol Venereol*. 2014. PMID: 25399481.
- **Design / N:** Randomized investigator-blind, parallel; 55 adult-acne patients, 9 months.
- **Intervention:** AzA 15% gel BID × 9 mo vs AzA 15% × 3 mo then adapalene 0.1% × 6 mo.
- **Outcome:** AzA monotherapy 9-mo arm showed **greater** reduction in inflammatory and total lesion counts than the switch arm; non-inferior to adapalene head-to-head in earlier sub-analyses.
- **Quality:** Moderate (Level 1b).
- **Takeaway:** AzA 15% gel sustained as monotherapy matches or exceeds adapalene for maintenance — supports keeping AzA as the workhorse, with adapalene as add-on rather than swap.

## 2. Azelaic Acid for Post-Inflammatory Hyperpigmentation (PIH) in Skin of Color

### Paper 3 — Lowe et al. 1998 (AzA 20% in phototypes IV-VI)
- **Citation:** Lowe NJ, Rizk D, Grimes P, Billips M, Pincus S. Azelaic acid 20% cream in the treatment of facial hyperpigmentation in darker-skinned patients. *Clin Ther*. 1998;20(5):945-959.
- **Design / N:** 24-week multicenter RCT, double-blind, parallel; N=52, Fitzpatrick IV-VI.
- **Intervention:** AzA 20% cream BID vs vehicle.
- **Outcome:** Significantly greater reduction in pigmentary intensity by both investigator scale and chromameter; significant global improvement at week 24.
- **Quality:** High (Level 1b).
- **Takeaway:** Directly applicable to a Fitzpatrick III-IV Korean male — AzA selectively inhibits hyperactive melanocytes without depigmenting normal skin (no rebound, no ochronosis risk).

### Paper 4 — Bagherani & Smoller 2023 / Sarkar comparison (AzA 20% vs tranexamic acid 5%)
- **Citation:** Tehranchinia Z, Saghi B, Rahimi H. A comparative study of 20% azelaic acid cream versus 5% tranexamic acid solution for the treatment of postinflammatory hyperpigmentation in patients with acne vulgaris: a single-blinded randomized clinical trial. *J Res Med Sci*. 2023;28:27. PMID: 37213446.
- **Design / N:** Single-blind RCT, split-face design, acne-PIH patients.
- **Intervention:** AzA 20% cream vs TA 5% topical solution, 12 weeks.
- **Outcome:** **Comparable efficacy** in PIH index reduction; TA had marginally better tolerability month 1.
- **Quality:** Moderate (Level 2b, single-blind).
- **Takeaway:** AzA 20% is a validated PIH treatment with equivalence to tranexamic acid — confirms it as evidence-based pigment therapy for acne aftermath.

## 3. Azelaic Acid for Rosacea / Persistent Erythema (Vascular Component)

### Paper 5 — Thiboutot et al. 2003 (AzA 15% gel pivotal phase III)
- **Citation:** Thiboutot D, Thieroff-Ekerdt R, Graupe K. Efficacy and safety of azelaic acid (15%) gel as a new treatment for papulopustular rosacea: results from two vehicle-controlled, randomized phase III studies. *J Am Acad Dermatol*. 2003;48(6):836-845. PMID: 12789172.
- **Design / N:** Two parallel double-blind RCTs, N=329 + 335 (total 664), 15 weeks.
- **Intervention:** AzA 15% gel BID vs vehicle.
- **Outcome:** Continuous significant reduction in inflammatory lesion count AND in erythema scores over 15 weeks (both p<0.001 vs vehicle).
- **Quality:** High (Level 1b, FDA pivotal).
- **Takeaway:** This is the strongest evidence that AzA reduces **erythema specifically**, not just papules — the mechanism transfers to PIE since both are vascular/inflammatory phenomena.

### Paper 6 — Liu et al. 2006 (systematic review of AzA in rosacea)
- **Citation:** Liu RH, Smith MK, Basta SA, Farmer ER. Azelaic acid in the treatment of papulopustular rosacea: a systematic review of randomized controlled trials. *Arch Dermatol*. 2006;142(8):1047-1052. PMID: 16924055.
- **Design / N:** Systematic review of 5 RCTs.
- **Intervention:** AzA 20% cream or 15% gel vs vehicle or metronidazole.
- **Outcome:** AzA significantly reduces inflammatory lesion count and erythema severity; equal or superior to metronidazole.
- **Quality:** High (Level 1a, systematic review of RCTs).
- **Takeaway:** Class-level evidence confirms AzA's anti-erythema effect; supports its use for the vascular PIE component in this patient.

## 4. Azelaic Acid for Melasma / Lentigines (vs Hydroquinone)

### Paper 7 — Verallo-Rowell et al. 1989 / Baliña & Graupe 1991 (AzA 20% vs HQ 4%)
- **Citation:** Baliña LM, Graupe K. The treatment of melasma: 20% azelaic acid versus 4% hydroquinone cream. *Int J Dermatol*. 1991;30(12):893-895. PMID: 1816137.
- **Design / N:** 24-week double-blind RCT, N=329 women with melasma.
- **Intervention:** AzA 20% cream vs HQ 4% cream, both with broad-spectrum SPF.
- **Outcome:** AzA 20% achieved 65% good/excellent results; **no significant difference vs HQ 4%** in overall rating, lesion size, or pigment intensity. No ochronosis or sensitization with AzA.
- **Quality:** High (Level 1b).
- **Takeaway:** AzA 20% matches HQ 4% on efficacy without HQ's long-term safety concerns (ochronosis, paradoxical darkening) — making it the safer long-term option for a 29-year-old who will use pigment treatment for years.

## 5. PIE / Post-Acne Erythema: Differentiation & Treatments

### Paper 8 — Liu et al. 2024 (AzA 15% gel for PIE + PIH — direct evidence)
- **Citation:** Liu D, Zhang X, Tan Y, et al. Effects of 15% azelaic acid gel in the management of post-inflammatory erythema and post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation in acne vulgaris. *Dermatol Ther (Heidelb)*. 2024;14(6):1605-1620. PMID: 38734843; PMC11116308.
- **Design / N:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled RCT; N=72 mild-moderate acne with PIE/PIH; 12 weeks.
- **Intervention:** AzA 15% gel BID vs AzA-free vehicle gel.
- **Outcome:** AzA significantly reduced **both** PIE (hemoglobin index by VISIA) AND PIH (melanin, PAHPI) vs placebo at week 12; no barrier disruption (TEWL stable); patient QoL improved.
- **Quality:** High (Level 1b — only direct PIE RCT for a topical).
- **Takeaway:** **This is the keystone paper for this patient's case.** It is the only RCT specifically validating a topical for PIE — and it shows AzA 15% works for the exact "red bruise-like marks + brown marks" presentation described.

### Paper 9 — Bae-Harboe & Graber 2013 (PIE differentiation review)
- **Citation:** Bae-Harboe YS, Graber EM. Easy as PIE (Postinflammatory Erythema). *J Clin Aesthet Dermatol*. 2013;6(9):46-47. PMC3780804.
- **Design:** Narrative clinical review.
- **Takeaway:** PIE blanches under **diascopy** (PIH does not); PIE is vascular not pigmentary — confirming the "appears when fatigued" pattern in this patient is consistent with PIE/vasoreactivity. First-line: topical AzA, niacinamide, vitamin C; Tier 2: PDL or IPL.

## 6. Pulsed Dye Laser (PDL) for PIE / Inflammatory Acne (Tier 2 Escalation)

### Paper 10 — Seaton et al. 2003 (PDL for inflammatory acne — Lancet RCT)
- **Citation:** Seaton ED, Charakida A, Mouser PE, Grace I, Clement RM, Chu AC. Pulsed-dye laser treatment for inflammatory acne vulgaris: randomised controlled trial. *Lancet*. 2003;362(9393):1347-1352. PMID: 14585635.
- **Design / N:** Double-blind RCT, N=41, single PDL session vs sham, 12-week follow-up.
- **Intervention:** 585-nm PDL once vs sham.
- **Outcome:** PDL significantly reduced inflammatory acne severity (Leeds score) vs sham at 12 weeks; no serious adverse events.
- **Quality:** High (Level 1b, Lancet).
- **Takeaway:** Single PDL session offers meaningful inflammatory-acne benefit — relevant if AzA monotherapy plateaus.

### Paper 11 — Alharbi et al. 2025 (PDL vs brimonidine vs combo for post-acne erythema)
- **Citation:** Alharbi MA et al. A split-face randomized comparative study of efficacy of pulsed dye laser, topical brimonidine tartrate, and their combination for treatment of post-acne erythema. *J Cosmet Dermatol*. 2025. PMID: 40833510.
- **Design / N:** Split-face RCT, N=60, post-acne erythema patients.
- **Intervention:** 595-nm PDL vs brimonidine 0.33% gel vs combo.
- **Outcome:** All arms improved significantly vs baseline; **PDL + brimonidine superior** on global assessment, lesion-count reduction, erythema index, and satisfaction.
- **Quality:** High (Level 1b, split-face controls confounders).
- **Takeaway:** PDL is validated specifically for post-acne erythema (PIE), with additive benefit when paired with a vasoconstrictor — clear Tier 2 if topicals plateau by month 3-4.

## Clinical Bottom Line for This Patient

1. **Tier 1 (start now):** AzA 15% gel BID (Liu 2024 direct PIE evidence + Lowe 1998 SoC PIH evidence + Thiboutot 2003 anti-erythema evidence). Single agent covers all three concerns.
2. **Tier 1 adjunct:** Daily broad-spectrum SPF 50, niacinamide 4-5% AM (synergistic on vascular reactivity), gentle ceramide moisturizer to offset AzA's mild stinging.
3. **Tier 1.5 (if active acne persists at 8-12 wk):** Add adapalene 0.1% gel QHS (Stinco non-inferiority data; AzA + retinoid is synergistic for pigment per JDD 2013 SoC review).
4. **Tier 2 (12-16 wk if PIE persists):** 595-nm PDL, 2-3 sessions 4 weeks apart, ± topical brimonidine for flushing days (Alharbi 2025).
5. **Avoid:** Hydroquinone long-term (ochronosis risk in Asian skin); BPO + clinda primary (Schaller showed marginal extra efficacy at cost of more irritation and no pigment benefit).
