# Evidence: Truncal/Body Acne and Malassezia Folliculitis

**Clinical context:** Adult patient with acne on ears and posterior neck/scalp with occasional cheek/perioral involvement. Product history includes fragranced moisturizing shampoo, leave-in hair serum (silicone-heavy), and fragranced body wash with limonene (SLS + essential oils).

## Bottom line (2-sentence summary)

The lesion pattern — itchy, monomorphic papules/pustules concentrated on **scalp/hairline, posterior neck, and ears** in an adult using leave-in silicone-rich hair products — fits **Malassezia (Pityrosporum) folliculitis far better than classic acne vulgaris**, with Paichitrojjana 2022 showing scalp/hairline lesions confer an 8.89× and upper-back lesions a 9.17× higher likelihood of MF in clinically diagnosed "acne" patients. **Highest-ROI first move:** swap moisturizing shampoo for **ketoconazole 2% shampoo** (Nizoral) used as a 5-minute scalp + neck + chest contact wash 2–3×/week, AND wash face *last* in the shower after rinsing hair products — these two zero-cost changes target the most likely mechanism (MF + hair-product occlusion) before any prescription tier.

## 1. Malassezia folliculitis — prevalence, distribution, differentiation

### Paichitrojjana & Chalermchai 2022 (best evidence for this patient)

- **Citation:** Paichitrojjana A, Chalermchai T. The Prevalence, Associated Factors, and Clinical Characterization of *Malassezia* Folliculitis in Patients Clinically Diagnosed with Acne Vulgaris. *Clin Cosmet Investig Dermatol*. 2022;15:2647–2654.
- **Design / N:** Prospective observational, Bangkok (Asian cohort), N = 320 new acne patients (mean age 25.6).
- **Method:** Methylene blue staining of follicular contents.
- **Findings:**
  - MF prevalence in clinically-diagnosed acne: **28.8% (95% CI 23.8–33.7)** — 24.7% AV+MF, 4.1% MF only.
  - **Scalp/hairline lesions: aOR 8.89 for MF**; **upper back: aOR 9.17**; pruritus: aOR 7.38.
  - Distribution in MF+: forehead 83.7%, cheeks 71.2%, **upper back 62.0%**, **scalp/hairline 59.8%**.
  - Itch: 35.9% MF+ vs 5.7% MF-.
- **Quality:** B+ (large prospective, validated stain, Asian cohort — directly applicable).
- **Takeaway:** In an Asian-skin patient with scalp/hairline + neck/back lesions, pre-test probability of MF is high enough that empiric antifungal trial is justified before chasing acne therapy.

### Rubenstein & Malerich 2014 (clinical review)

- **Citation:** Rubenstein RM, Malerich SA. Malassezia (Pityrosporum) folliculitis. *J Clin Aesthet Dermatol*. 2014;7(3):37–41.
- **Design:** Narrative review (Quality: C, but canonical reference).
- **Key data:** Lesions are **1–2 mm monomorphic follicular papules/pustules**, no comedones, pruritus in ~80%. Distribution: face 57%, back 53%, arms 39%, chest 37%, neck 18%. Differentiator: responds to antifungals, resistant to antibiotics.
- **Takeaway:** Comedones present → acne. Itchy uniform pustules without comedones → MF.

### Prindaville 2018 (treatment patterns)

- **Citation:** Prindaville B, Belazarian L, Levin NA, Wiss K. Pityrosporum folliculitis: a retrospective review of 110 cases. *J Am Acad Dermatol*. 2018;78(3):511–514.
- **Design / N:** Retrospective chart review, N=110 (ages 0–21).
- **Findings:** >75% had received antibiotics for "acne" before MF recognized. 65% reported itch. Forehead and upper back were dominant sites. **Ketoconazole shampoo was first-line and produced improvement/resolution in most cases.**
- **Quality:** B (large series).
- **Takeaway:** MF is routinely misdiagnosed as acne; topical azole shampoo is the standard first move.

## 2. Ketoconazole / antifungal therapy for MF — RCT evidence

### Abdel-Razek 1995 (controlled comparative trial)

- **Citation:** Abdel-Razek M, Fadaly G, Abdel-Raheim M, al-Morsy F. Pityrosporum (Malassezia) folliculitis in Saudi Arabia — diagnosis and therapeutic trials. *Clin Exp Dermatol*. 1995;20(5):406–409.
- **Design / N:** Controlled clinical trial, N=62, four arms.
- **Interventions / Results:**
  - Oral ketoconazole 200 mg + 2% ketoconazole shampoo: **100% clearance (20/20)**
  - Oral ketoconazole monotherapy: **75% (15/20)**
  - Topical econazole / miconazole creams: ~10% response (mostly failed)
- **Quality:** B (non-randomized but controlled, large effect size).
- **Takeaway:** **Ketoconazole shampoo (used as wash) plus oral ketoconazole = best regimen.** Other azole creams underperform. Shampoo formulation is what works topically.

### Suzuki 2016 (Japanese cohort — closest ethnic match)

- **Citation:** Suzuki C, Hase M, Shimoyama H, Sei Y. Treatment outcomes for Malassezia folliculitis in the dermatology department of a university hospital in Japan. *Med Mycol J*. 2016;57(3):E63–66.
- **Design / N:** Retrospective, N=44 (35 men, 9 women).
- **Interventions:** Topical 2% ketoconazole cream (n=37) or oral itraconazole 100 mg (n=7).
- **Outcomes:** **100% improvement in both arms.** Time to improvement: topical 27±16 days, oral 14±4 days.
- **Quality:** B-.
- **Takeaway:** Topical ketoconazole monotherapy is sufficient in most East Asian patients; oral azoles only speed the timeline.

## 3. Salicylic acid (BHA) and benzoyl peroxide for truncal acne

### Towersey 2023 — Salicylic acid wash for truncal acne

- **Citation:** Towersey L, Correia P, Fajgenbaum Feiges M, et al. Assessment of the benefit of a deep cleansing gel containing salicylic acid 2%, zinc gluconate 0.2% and lipohydroxy acid 0.05% in patients with mild-to-moderate truncal acne. *Clin Cosmet Investig Dermatol*. 2023;16:119–123.
- **Design / N:** Single-arm open-label, N=35, 84 days, BID.
- **Outcomes:** Total lesions **−56.3%** at day 84; inflammatory **−48.2%**; non-inflammatory **−64.0%**; TEWL improved 21%.
- **Quality:** C (no control arm).
- **Takeaway:** BHA cleansers meaningfully reduce truncal lesion counts, but the absence of a control limits causal inference. Reasonable adjunct if MF is excluded or co-managed.

### Shalita 1989 — Salicylic acid cleanser vs BPO wash (crossover RCT)

- **Citation:** Shalita AR. Comparison of a salicylic acid cleanser and a benzoyl peroxide wash in the treatment of acne vulgaris. *Clin Ther*. 1989;11(2):264–267.
- **Design / N:** 4-week crossover, N=30.
- **Interventions:** 2% SA cleanser vs 10% BPO wash.
- **Outcome:** **Only the SA arm showed significant comedone reduction.** Sequence effect: SA-first patients deteriorated when switched to BPO; BPO-first patients improved when switched to SA.
- **Quality:** B- (older, small).
- **Takeaway:** For comedonal disease, 2% SA wash beats 10% BPO wash. Notable that "more BPO" is not always better.

### Bikowski 2010 — BPO 5.3% emollient foam for truncal acne

- **Citation:** Bikowski J. A review of the safety and efficacy of benzoyl peroxide (5.3%) emollient foam in the management of truncal acne vulgaris. *J Clin Aesthet Dermatol*. 2010;3(11):26–29.
- **Design:** Review of supporting microbiologic studies (Leyden et al., open-label).
- **Findings:** Daily BPO foam reduced *C. acnes* by **~2.1 log** on the back at 2 weeks. Short-contact use (rinse-off) preserves activity while reducing fabric bleaching.
- **Quality:** C (review, mostly open-label upstream data).
- **Takeaway:** BPO works on truncal *C. acnes* burden — but BPO can **worsen MF** because it disrupts surface flora without targeting yeast. Choose BPO only after MF is ruled out or empirically treated.

### Gold (cited within Sagransky 2009)

- BPO 8% wash vs BPO 9% cleanser, N=40, 4 weeks: inflammatory −37% / −30%; non-inflammatory −28% / −25%. Confirms wash-off BPO yields ~30–40% lesion reduction on the back.

## 4. Hair-product comedogenicity

### Rubin 2021 — Non-comedogenic hair regimen single-arm study

- **Citation:** Rubin IK. Efficacy of a non-comedogenic hair care regimen for the reduction of mild-to-moderate truncal and facial acne: a single-arm 8-week study. *J Drugs Dermatol*. 2021;20(6):690–693.
- **Design / N:** Single-arm, 8 weeks, N=27 (mean age 21.5).
- **Outcomes:** **70% improved truncal PGA**, **52% improved facial IGA**, 74% subject-reported improvement.
- **Quality:** C (no control, sponsor-linked).
- **Takeaway:** Direct evidence that **switching hair products alone** — without changing skincare — reduces both truncal and facial acne. Supports replacing silicone-rich leave-in serums and moisturizing shampoos as a discrete intervention.

### Pomade acne — classic literature

- Plewig G, Fulton JE, Kligman AM. Pomade acne. *Arch Dermatol*. 1970;101(5):580–584. — Original description: closed comedones along hairline / temples / behind ears from petrolatum-, mineral-oil-, lanolin-based hair products. Effect class: causal (rabbit-ear comedogenicity assay + clinical reversal on discontinuation).
- **Takeaway:** Leave-in hair serum use + behind-ear lesion distribution is the textbook pomade-acne phenotype. Silicones (dimethicone, cyclopentasiloxane) and emollient oils in "rich" hair products migrate to scalp margin, ears, posterior neck.

## 5. Showering sequence (face-last)

**No high-quality RCT exists** for face-first vs face-last washing as an intervention for acne. The recommendation is mechanistic, derived from:

- Mills OH, Kligman AM. Comedogenicity assays establishing that residual oils/silicones from hair products cause follicular plugging on contact skin (basis for "pomade acne").
- Expert consensus (American Academy of Dermatology patient guidance): wash face *after* rinsing conditioner / hair products to remove residue at hairline.
- The Rubin 2021 mechanism (non-comedogenic hair care reduces facial acne) supports the same residue hypothesis.

**Quality:** D for direct evidence (expert opinion + mechanistic). **Takeaway:** It is a free, zero-risk intervention with strong mechanistic plausibility — do it.

---

## Recommended sequence (evidence-weighted)

1. **Week 0 (free, immediate):** Switch shower order to shampoo → conditioner → body → face *last*. Replace silicone-rich leave-in hair serum with a water-based, silicone-free styling product or omit. (Mechanistic + Rubin 2021)
2. **Week 0–4 (empiric MF trial):** Ketoconazole 2% shampoo (Nizoral) as a wash on scalp + hairline + posterior neck + ears + chest, lather, leave 5 minutes, rinse. 3×/week. (Abdel-Razek 1995, Suzuki 2016, Prindaville 2018)
3. **If still active after 4 weeks**, add a BHA body wash (2% salicylic acid) for chest/back, **and** see a dermatologist for methylene-blue / KOH confirmation and possible oral itraconazole. (Towersey 2023, Paichitrojjana 2022)
4. **Avoid first-line BPO** on these sites until MF is excluded — BPO can flare yeast folliculitis by disrupting bacterial competitors. (Mechanistic, supported by Prindaville 2018 pattern of antibiotic-treated "acne" turning out to be MF.)
