# Evidence: Solar Lentigines & Post-UV Hyperpigmentation (Fitzpatrick III–IV)

## Summary (efficacy × evidence quality × safety ranking)

For new, discrete solar lentigines after acute high-UV exposure in a Fitzpatrick III–IV adult patient, the single-best evidence-backed clearance modality is a **picosecond 532 nm Nd:YAG / KTP laser** (≥75% clearance in 90–93% of lesions in Asian-skin prospective studies, with PIH rates ~5–10% — far lower than Q-switched lasers). The strongest *topical* combination is **oral tranexamic acid 250 mg BID × 3 months** (49% mMASI reduction in a placebo-controlled RCT) layered on **topical antioxidants — 15% L-ascorbic acid + 1% α-tocopherol + 0.5% ferulic acid (CEF) every morning for prevention** plus **niacinamide 5%** and **hydroquinone 4% short-course (8–12 wk only)** for cycling depigmentation; with **continued SPF50+ PA++++** as the non-negotiable substrate. The picosecond laser is the *efficacy* winner; CEF + sunscreen is the *prevention* winner; oral TXA is the systemic add-on if topicals plateau — IPL is a reasonable lower-cost alternative if pico is not available, and Q-switched Nd:YAG is acceptable but carries the highest PIH risk in Fitzpatrick IV Asian skin.

## 1. Topical Vitamin C (L-ascorbic acid) for facial pigmentation — RCTs

### Paper 1 — Espinal-Pérez et al. 2004 (5% AA vs 4% HQ, split-face RCT)
- **Citation:** Espinal-Perez LE, Moncada B, Castanedo-Cazares JP. A double-blind randomized trial of 5% ascorbic acid vs. 4% hydroquinone in melasma. *Int J Dermatol*. 2004;43(8):604-607. DOI: 10.1111/j.1365-4632.2004.02134.x. PMID: 15304189.
- **Design / N:** Double-blind, randomized, split-face; N = 16 women with idiopathic melasma; 16 weeks.
- **Intervention vs comparator:** 5% L-ascorbic acid cream (one cheek) vs 4% hydroquinone cream (other cheek), nightly, plus daily sunscreen.
- **Outcome:** Subjective good/excellent improvement HQ 93% vs AA 62.5% (p<0.05); colorimetry showed **no significant difference** between sides; side effects HQ 68.7% vs AA 6.2%.
- **Quality grade:** Moderate (Level 1b, small N).
- **Takeaway:** Topical L-ascorbic acid is meaningfully effective but slightly less potent than HQ — its dramatically lower irritation makes it appropriate for chronic daytime use, while HQ is reserved for short bursts.

### Paper 2 — Correia & Magina 2023 (Topical vitamin C SR)
- **Citation:** Correia G, Magina S. Efficacy of topical vitamin C in melasma and photoaging: A systematic review. *J Cosmet Dermatol*. 2023;22(7):1938-1945. DOI: 10.1111/jocd.15748. PMID: 37128827.
- **Design / N:** Systematic review of 7 RCTs, 139 participants total.
- **Intervention vs comparator:** Topical vitamin C (various concentrations/derivatives) vs vehicle, HQ, or other actives, including QS Nd:YAG-adjunct protocols.
- **Outcome:** Significant lightening of pigmentation on objective measures; effects modest, slow-onset; QS Nd:YAG + topical vitamin C combinations enhanced response.
- **Quality grade:** Moderate-high (Cochrane-graded SR of small RCTs).
- **Takeaway:** Vitamin C is best used as a daily preventive antioxidant and laser adjunct rather than a standalone "spot eraser" — supports a 10–20% L-AA morning serum.

## 2. Vitamin C + E + Ferulic acid (CEF) photoprotection — Lin/Pinnell program

### Paper 3 — Lin et al. 2003 (combination C+E vs single agents)
- **Citation:** Lin JY, Selim MA, Shea CR, Grichnik JM, Omar MM, Monteiro-Riviere NA, Pinnell SR. UV photoprotection by combination topical antioxidants vitamin C and vitamin E. *J Am Acad Dermatol*. 2003;48(6):866-874. DOI: 10.1067/mjd.2003.425. PMID: 12789176.
- **Design / N:** Controlled animal/in-vivo study; pig skin (human-resembling), solar-simulated UV 1–5× MED; 4 days of application.
- **Intervention vs comparator:** 15% L-ascorbic acid + 1% α-tocopherol vs C alone, vs E alone, vs vehicle.
- **Outcome:** Combination significantly more protective than either monotherapy; antioxidant protection factor ~4× after 4 days; protection against erythema, sunburn cells, and thymine dimers.
- **Quality grade:** High (foundational mechanistic, controlled).
- **Takeaway:** Establishes the C+E combination rationale — neither alone is sufficient; this is the scientific basis for layering a 15% vitamin C + 1% vitamin E serum.

### Paper 4 — Murray, Burch, Pinnell et al. 2008 (CEF in human skin)
- **Citation:** Murray JC, Burch JA, Streilein RD, Iannacchione MA, Hall RP, Pinnell SR. A topical antioxidant solution containing vitamins C and E stabilized by ferulic acid provides protection for human skin against damage caused by ultraviolet irradiation. *J Am Acad Dermatol*. 2008;59(3):418-425. DOI: 10.1016/j.jaad.2008.05.004. PMID: 18603326.
- **Design / N:** In-vivo human study; patches treated 4 days, irradiated 2–10× MED.
- **Intervention vs comparator:** 15% L-AA + 1% α-tocopherol + 0.5% ferulic acid (CEFer / SkinCeuticals CEF formula) vs vehicle.
- **Outcome:** Significant reduction in erythema, sunburn cells, thymine dimers, p53; suppressed UV-induced IL-1α, IL-6, IL-8, IL-10, TNF-α.
- **Quality grade:** High (human in-vivo, vehicle-controlled).
- **Takeaway:** CEF works **on top of** sunscreen via a different mechanism (antioxidant scavenging rather than UV filtering) — strong rationale for daily AM use after any high-UV exposure event and as standing maintenance to prevent new lentigines.

### Paper 5 — Wu et al. 2013 (CEF in Chinese skin)
- **Citation:** Wu Y, Zheng X, Xu XG, Li YH, Wang B, Gao XH, Chen HD, Yatskayer M, Oresajo C. Protective effects of a topical antioxidant complex containing vitamins C and E and ferulic acid against ultraviolet irradiation-induced photodamage in Chinese women. *J Drugs Dermatol*. 2013;12(4):464-468. PMID: 23652896.
- **Design / N:** Controlled in-vivo study, N = 12 Chinese women; 5× MED ssUVR; 4-day pre-treatment.
- **Intervention vs comparator:** CEF antioxidant complex vs vehicle vs positive control vs negative control.
- **Outcome:** Significant suppression of sunburn-cell formation, thymine dimer formation, p53 overexpression, and CD1a+ Langerhans cell depletion in CEF-treated skin.
- **Quality grade:** Moderate (small N; same patient design).
- **Takeaway:** Demonstrates CEF efficacy specifically in East-Asian skin — directly transferable evidence to a Fitzpatrick III–IV patient with similar phototype.

## 3. Topical Tranexamic Acid for melasma/lentigines

### Paper 6 — Chung, Lee, Lee 2016 (topical TXA + IPL split-face RCT, Korean)
- **Citation:** Chung JY, Lee JH, Lee JH. Topical tranexamic acid as an adjuvant treatment in melasma: Side-by-side comparison clinical study. *J Dermatolog Treat*. 2016;27(4):373-377. DOI: 10.3109/09546634.2015.1115812. PMID: 26526300.
- **Design / N:** Randomized, split-face (internally controlled) trial; N = 15 Asian women; 4 monthly IPL sessions; 12-week follow-up.
- **Intervention vs comparator:** Topical TXA + IPL on one side vs vehicle + IPL on the other side.
- **Outcome:** Both melanin index (Mexameter) and mMASI decreased significantly on the topical-TXA side but **not** on vehicle; topical TXA significantly reduced rebound pigmentation after IPL.
- **Quality grade:** Moderate (small N, but split-face self-controlled).
- **Takeaway:** Topical TXA is a useful **rebound-PIH preventer** when stacking IPL or pico onto Asian skin — directly relevant if the patient pursues device-based clearance.

### Paper 7 — Calacattawi et al. 2024 (TXA RCT meta-analysis, 22 RCTs / 1,280 patients)
- **Citation:** Calacattawi R, Alshahrani M, Aleid M, et al. Tranexamic acid as a therapeutic option for melasma management: meta-analysis and systematic review of randomized controlled trials. *J Dermatolog Treat*. 2024;35(1):2361106. DOI: 10.1080/09546634.2024.2361106. PMID: 38843906.
- **Design / N:** PROSPERO/PRISMA meta-analysis; 22 RCTs, N = 1,280; oral, topical, and injectable TXA arms.
- **Intervention vs comparator:** TXA (any route) vs placebo, HQ, or combination treatments.
- **Outcome:** Significant reductions in MASI, mMASI, MI, hemi-MASI across routes; **oral TXA produced the largest MASI reduction, followed by injection then topical**. High heterogeneity for combined protocols.
- **Quality grade:** High (Level 1a; high heterogeneity noted).
- **Takeaway:** Ranks routes — if topical TXA underperforms, escalation to oral TXA is evidence-supported; topical alone is the weakest TXA modality.

### Paper 8 — Bala, Lee, Pandya, Rodrigues 2018 (Oral TXA review)
- **Citation:** Bala HR, Lee S, Wong C, Pandya AG, Rodrigues M. Oral Tranexamic Acid for the Treatment of Melasma: A Review. *Dermatol Surg*. 2018;44(6):814-825. DOI: 10.1097/DSS.0000000000001518. PMID: 29677015.
- **Design / N:** Comprehensive narrative review of oral TXA literature.
- **Intervention vs comparator:** Oral TXA 500 mg/day (typical 250 mg BID), 8–12 weeks vs other modalities or placebo.
- **Outcome:** Consistent efficacy in Asian skin even at low doses over short periods; no increased thromboembolic risk in screened patients; mild GI/menstrual side effects only.
- **Quality grade:** Moderate (narrative review, but the authoritative reference for oral TXA dosing).
- **Takeaway:** 250 mg BID × 8–12 weeks is the evidence-backed starter regimen; screen for personal/family thromboembolism history first — recommendable but should be MD-supervised.

## 4. Oral Tranexamic Acid — pivotal RCT

### Paper 9 — Del Rosario, Pandya et al. 2018 (RCT, placebo-controlled)
- **Citation:** Del Rosario E, Florez-Pollack S, Zapata L Jr, Hernandez K, Tovar-Garza A, Rodrigues M, Hynan LS, Pandya AG. Randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind study of oral tranexamic acid in the treatment of moderate-to-severe melasma. *J Am Acad Dermatol*. 2018;78(2):363-369. DOI: 10.1016/j.jaad.2017.09.053. PMID: 28987494.
- **Design / N:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled RCT; N = 44 enrolled, 39 completed; 3 months treatment + 3 months follow-up.
- **Intervention vs comparator:** Oral TXA 250 mg BID + sunscreen vs placebo + sunscreen.
- **Outcome:** **49% mMASI reduction** in TXA arm vs 18% in placebo at 3 months (p significant); severe melasma improved more than moderate; partial rebound at 3 months post-treatment (TXA 26% vs placebo 19% from baseline). No serious adverse events.
- **Quality grade:** High (Level 1b, the cleanest placebo-controlled oral TXA RCT).
- **Takeaway:** The most rigorous evidence for oral TXA — large effect size, acceptable safety; this is the trial that justifies prescribing 250 mg BID for 12 weeks if topicals plateau.

## 5. Hydroquinone 4% — the historical gold standard

### Paper 10 — Jarratt 2004 (Mequinol 2%/tretinoin 0.01% vs HQ 3% for lentigines)
- **Citation:** Jarratt M. Mequinol 2%/tretinoin 0.01% solution: an effective and safe alternative to hydroquinone 3% in the treatment of solar lentigines. *Cutis*. 2004;74(5):319-322. PMID: 15605971.
- **Design / N:** Randomized, parallel-group, double-masked study; N = 216; 16-week treatment, 24-week follow-up.
- **Intervention vs comparator:** Mequinol 2% + tretinoin 0.01% vs HQ 3% vs vehicle vs active components alone — BID × 16 weeks, solar-lentigines target.
- **Outcome:** Mequinol/tretinoin clinical success significantly higher than HQ 3% on forearm; on face, mequinol/tretinoin "consistently higher" than HQ 3% (trend favouring at week 40). All groups: mild/transient adverse events only.
- **Quality grade:** High (Level 1b — large N, double-masked).
- **Takeaway:** HQ 3–4% remains a benchmark but is no longer clearly the best topical for solar lentigines; mequinol/tretinoin and modern alternatives match or beat it. Reserve HQ 4% for **8–12 week courses only** to avoid ochronosis; do not chronically use.

### Paper 11 — Makino et al. 2013 (HQ 4% vs SMA-432 multimodal brightener)
- **Citation:** Makino ET, Mehta RC, Garruto J, Gotz V, Sigler ML, Herndon JH. Clinical efficacy and safety of a multimodality skin brightener composition compared with 4% hydroquinone. *J Drugs Dermatol*. 2013;12(3):s21-26. PMID: 23545929.
- **Design / N:** Randomized, double-blind, split-face study; N = 68 women; 12 weeks.
- **Intervention vs comparator:** 3 SMA-432 (PGE2-inhibitor) brightener formulations vs HQ 4%.
- **Outcome:** Non-HQ formulations as effective as HQ 4% for facial hyperpigmentation, with HQ 4% rated **least favourable** by subjects for tolerability.
- **Quality grade:** Moderate-high (industry-sponsored but well-controlled).
- **Takeaway:** Non-HQ multi-actives can match HQ 4% — supports a stack approach (TXA + niacinamide + vitamin C + retinoid) over chronic HQ.

## 6. Picosecond laser (532 nm + 1064 nm) — Asian-skin RCT/prospective evidence

### Paper 12 — Negishi, Akita, Matsunaga 2018 (532 nm pico Nd:YAG in Asians)
- **Citation:** Negishi K, Akita H, Matsunaga Y. Prospective study of removing solar lentigines in Asians using a novel dual-wavelength and dual-pulse width picosecond laser. *Lasers Surg Med*. 2018;50(8):851-858. DOI: 10.1002/lsm.22820. PMID: 29608215.
- **Design / N:** Prospective IRB-approved trial; 20 Japanese women, Fitzpatrick III–IV; 43 facial lentigines.
- **Intervention:** 532 nm 750 ps Nd:YAG; 0.35 ± 0.06 J/cm² average fluence; 3–4 mm spot.
- **Outcome:** **40/43 lesions (93.0%) ≥75% clearance after one treatment**; remaining 3 cleared with a second pass; PIH only 4.65%; mean MI improvement 77.6% (1 mo) / 76.9% (3 mo).
- **Quality grade:** High (Level 2; well-controlled prospective in target skin type).
- **Takeaway:** The single best efficacy/safety datapoint for a Fitzpatrick III–IV patient — one to two pico sessions can clear >90% of new lentigines with low PIH risk.

### Paper 13 — Vachiramon et al. 2022 (532 nm KTP vs 755 nm alexandrite pico RCT)
- **Citation:** Vachiramon V, Namasondhi A, Anuntrangsee T, Jurairattanaporn N. Randomized, evaluator-blinded comparative study of a potassium titanyl phosphate (KTP) 532-nm picosecond laser and an alexandrite 755-nm picosecond laser for the treatment of solar lentigines in Asians. *J Cosmet Dermatol*. 2022;21(10):4370-4377. DOI: 10.1111/jocd.14831. PMID: 35129875.
- **Design / N:** Evaluator-blinded RCT; 30 Asian patients, 30 paired lentiginous lesions; single treatment; 12-week follow-up.
- **Intervention vs comparator:** KTP 532 nm pico vs alexandrite 755 nm pico, paired.
- **Outcome:** Both produced significant L* improvement; 755 nm alex pico **superior at 12 weeks** (>50% improvement: 25 lesions [alex] vs 19 [KTP 532]); satisfaction higher with 755 nm; adverse events similar.
- **Quality grade:** High (Level 1b, evaluator-blinded paired RCT).
- **Takeaway:** When both wavelengths are available, the 755 nm alexandrite pico is the slightly better single-session choice for solar lentigines in Asian skin.

### Paper 14 — Chan, Shek, Yeung, Chan 2019 (532 nm pico, Hong Kong cohort)
- **Citation:** Chan MWM, Shek SY, Yeung CK, Chan HH. A Prospective Study in the Treatment of Lentigines in Asian Skin Using 532 nm Picosecond Nd:YAG Laser. *Lasers Surg Med*. 2019;51(9):767-773. DOI: 10.1002/lsm.23103. PMID: 31115070.
- **Design / N:** Prospective open-label cohort; N = 20 Asian, Fitzpatrick III–IV; 89 facial lentigines; up to 3 sessions, 12-week post-treatment endpoint.
- **Intervention:** 532 nm 750 ps Nd:YAG; 0.2–0.5 J/cm²; 4 mm spot.
- **Outcome:** 18/20 patients (90%) moderate-to-significant improvement on PGA at 12 weeks; relative MI improved 33.3% → 37.6%; **PIH only 10.2% of sessions**; hypopigmentation 3.6%.
- **Quality grade:** Moderate-high (Level 2).
- **Takeaway:** Confirms 532 nm pico safety/efficacy specifically in Fitzpatrick III–IV; PIH is a real but manageable risk — pair with peri-procedural topical TXA + niacinamide to minimize it.

## 7. IPL (intense pulsed light) for solar lentigines

### Paper 15 — Mardani et al. 2025 (Systematic review of solar lentigines treatments)
- **Citation:** Mardani G, Nasiri MJ, Namazi N, Farshchian M, Abdollahimajd F. Treatment of Solar Lentigines: A Systematic Review of Clinical Trials. *J Cosmet Dermatol*. 2025;24(4):e70133. DOI: 10.1111/jocd.70133. PMID: 40145274.
- **Design / N:** Systematic review (Cochrane-graded); 41 clinical trials; N = 3,234 patients aged 24–92.
- **Intervention vs comparator:** Topicals, peels, cryotherapy, PDL, IPL, QS lasers, picosecond lasers, fractional CO₂.
- **Outcome (clearance rates):** Mequinol 2% + tretinoin 0.01% 52.6–80%+; **IPL 74.6–90%**; QS laser 36.4–76.6%; **picosecond 67.9–93.0%**; PDL 27–57%; fractional CO₂ 8–23%; cryotherapy 37–71.4%; TCA peels 12–46%. PIH lowest with PDL and IPL; cryotherapy worst.
- **Quality grade:** High (current SR).
- **Takeaway:** **IPL and picosecond are statistically the two highest-clearance modalities for solar lentigines**, with IPL having the better PIH profile — a strong evidence-based justification for choosing IPL or pico over cryotherapy or chemical peels.

## 8. Q-switched Nd:YAG vs alternatives in Asian skin

### Paper 16 — Jun, Kim et al. 2014 (QS Nd:YAG vs Er:YAG micropeel, split-face Korean RCT)
- **Citation:** Jun HJ, Kim SM, Choi WJ, Cho SH, Lee JD, Kim HS. A split-face, evaluator-blind randomized study on the early effects of Q-switched Nd:YAG laser versus Er:YAG micropeel in light solar lentigines in Asians. *J Cosmet Laser Ther*. 2014;16(2):83-88. DOI: 10.3109/14764172.2013.877749. PMID: 24409833.
- **Design / N:** Split-face, evaluator-blind randomized; N = 15 Korean patients; single session; 1-month follow-up.
- **Intervention vs comparator:** QS Nd:YAG (one side) vs Er:YAG micropeel (other side).
- **Outcome:** QS Nd:YAG produced greater pigment reduction at 2 weeks (p < 0.001), but by 1 month differences narrowed (p = 0.110) due to **higher PIH in the QS Nd:YAG side**.
- **Quality grade:** Moderate (small N, single session).
- **Takeaway:** QS Nd:YAG works but **PIH after QS in Asian skin is a real downside** — picosecond and IPL outperform it on safety; reserve QS Nd:YAG for cases where neither pico nor IPL is accessible.

## 9. Niacinamide for hyperpigmentation — Bissett/Hakozaki program

### Paper 17 — Hakozaki et al. 2002 (5% niacinamide split-face vs vehicle, Japanese)
- **Citation:** Hakozaki T, Minwalla L, Zhuang J, Chhoa M, Matsubara A, Miyamoto K, Greatens A, Hillebrand GG, Bissett DL, Boissy RE. The effect of niacinamide on reducing cutaneous pigmentation and suppression of melanosome transfer. *Br J Dermatol*. 2002;147(1):20-31. DOI: 10.1046/j.1365-2133.2002.04834.x. PMID: 12100180.
- **Design / N:** Two clinical studies — (i) N = 18 paired-design with 5% niacinamide moisturizer vs vehicle; (ii) N = 120 with 2% niacinamide + sunscreen vs sunscreen vs vehicle in facial-tanning subjects.
- **Intervention vs comparator:** Topical niacinamide (2–5%) vs vehicle / sunscreen.
- **Outcome:** Significant reduction in facial hyperpigmentation and increased skin lightness at 4 weeks (5% arm); 35–68% inhibition of melanosome transfer in melanocyte–keratinocyte coculture model.
- **Quality grade:** High (Level 1b for clinical, mechanistic in vitro).
- **Takeaway:** Niacinamide inhibits **melanosome transfer** (not melanin synthesis) — a complementary mechanism to vitamin C and TXA, making 5% niacinamide a logical bolt-on with no irritation penalty.

### Paper 18 — Bissett et al. 2004/2005 (5% niacinamide for photoaging signs)
- **Citation:** Bissett DL, Miyamoto K, Sun P, Li J, Berge CA. Topical niacinamide reduces yellowing, wrinkling, red blotchiness, and hyperpigmented spots in aging facial skin. *Int J Cosmet Sci*. 2004;26(5):231-238. DOI: 10.1111/j.1467-2494.2004.00228.x. PMID: 18492135.
- **Design / N:** 12-week, double-blind, placebo-controlled, split-face, left-right randomized; N = 50.
- **Intervention vs comparator:** Moisturizer + 5% niacinamide vs moisturizer alone.
- **Outcome:** Significant reductions in hyperpigmented spots, fine lines/wrinkles, red blotchiness, and yellowing vs control; well tolerated.
- **Quality grade:** High (Level 1b, well-powered split-face RCT).
- **Takeaway:** 5% niacinamide is the best-supported "anti-spot" cosmeceutical for daily AM/PM use and combines safely with vitamin C, TXA, retinoids, and sunscreen — a logical addition to the daily routine.

## Practical Synthesis

1. **Continue SPF50+ PA++++** daily, generously reapplied — non-negotiable, the substrate for everything else.
2. **AM:** 15% L-AA + 1% α-tocopherol + 0.5% ferulic acid (CEF-class) serum → 5% niacinamide → sunscreen. Evidence: Lin 2003, Murray/Pinnell 2008, Wu 2013, Hakozaki 2002, Bissett 2004.
3. **PM (8–12 wk cycle):** HQ 4% spot-only on individual lentigines (Espinal-Pérez 2004, Jarratt 2004, Makino 2013) — *or*, preferred, switch HQ for **topical TXA 3–5%** + retinoid (mequinol/tretinoin or tretinoin 0.025%) per Jarratt 2004 + Chung 2016. Avoid HQ >12 weeks continuously.
4. **If topicals plateau at 12 weeks:** add **oral TXA 250 mg BID × 12 weeks** under MD supervision (screen thromboembolic risk) — Del Rosario 2018, Bala 2018, Calacattawi 2024.
5. **Definitive clearance:** 1–2 sessions of **picosecond 532 nm Nd:YAG or 755 nm alexandrite** at a dermatology clinic (Negishi 2018: 93% ≥75% clearance, PIH 4.7%; Vachiramon 2022: 755 nm slightly superior to 532 nm). Use peri-procedural topical TXA + niacinamide to prevent rebound (Chung 2016). **IPL is the acceptable fallback** with comparable clearance (Mardani 2025 SR: 74.6–90%) and lower PIH risk; avoid QS Nd:YAG and cryotherapy as first-line in Fitzpatrick III-IV Asian skin.
